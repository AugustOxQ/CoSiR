## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat): the panel has to be satisfied that the planned study would produce evidence that means what the authors will say it means. That covers the design, how data are kept apart between development and testing, which statistics will be reported and how, and whether another group could rerun the work from what is written. At this stage the panel reviews a plan before its results exist, so missing numbers are not a defect in themselves. What counts is whether each candidate, its matched control, the bars, the kill and carry rules and the fresh-seed test can actually tell apart the outcomes they are meant to tell apart, with no information leaking from evaluation data into design choices, and within the time the authors have. Editorially this is the gate. A fatal problem here ends the submission, and a block or a warn here is enough on its own to send the plan back for major revision. I do not score this dimension; the methodology seat does.

D2 (writing_and_structure, normal priority, owned by my seat): the document has to be organised and written so that a reader outside the project can find, understand and apply what is being committed to, and it has to follow the conventions readers expect of a method-focused paper and, for a plan of this kind, of a pre-registration. For a pre-results plan I read this as four things. The binding commitments are stated once, clearly, in a place the reader can find. Terms are defined before they carry weight. Supporting or appended context is visibly subordinate to the plan. Confirmatory and exploratory content are kept apart. This dimension is not mandatory, so on its own it cannot force rejection or major revision. A warn or anything worse here still rules out clean acceptance and calls for at least minor revision, and acceptance requires every dimension, this one included, to pass.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: writing_and_structure
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Whether a reader new to the project can find, in one clearly marked place in the main plan, the question under test, each candidate with its matched control, the numeric bars, the kill and carry rules, the fresh-seed test and the order in which they are applied; whether every project-specific term, metric and label is defined at first use and used in one sense throughout; whether any appended or supporting material is labelled as context, with a stated rule for which text wins when it disagrees with the plan; whether pre-registered elements are visibly separated from results already observed or exploratory; and whether the document follows method-paper and pre-registration conventions (hypotheses, primary outcome, analysis plan, decision table, deviation policy, timeline), judged against those generic conventions rather than any venue's criteria.
what_triggers_block: The decision rule, a numeric bar, or a kill or carry rule is stated in more than one place with conflicting thresholds, metrics, seed sets or ordering, or rests on a load-bearing term that is undefined or used in two incompatible senses, or the text does not let a reader tell which tests are confirmatory and which are exploratory, and the document names no binding version, so two careful readers applying the plan to the same future results could reach different verdicts.
what_triggers_warn: The binding rule can be reconstructed without ambiguity but only by piecing it together from scattered sections or appended material, or rules are restated in wording that differs but does not conflict, or the confirmatory and exploratory split is labelled but inconsistently, or terms that do not drive the decision are defined late or not at all, or there is no single summary table of candidates, controls, bars and outcomes, so a reader needs clearly more effort than a self-contained pre-registration would ask of them.
