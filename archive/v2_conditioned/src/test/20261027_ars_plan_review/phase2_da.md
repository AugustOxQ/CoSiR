## Review Body

I read the plan as a CVPR method paper whose core thesis has three parts. C1 is a new task in which the respect of similarity is fixed only by cross-item example pairs. C2 is a shared sparse image–text factor basis, trained on pseudo-aspect episodes, that makes four example pairs enough. C3 is an analysis claim that aspects live asymmetrically across modalities. Following the contract amendment, I did not count missing results against the plan. For each claim I asked whether the planned design could refute it, and whether the appended evidence, negative results included, points toward it or away from it. Section labels such as "(approved)" and the prepared answers to anticipated objections (literature review §5) are author assertions. I checked each one against the plan body and did not treat it as settled. The manuscript contains no instruction aimed at reviewers.

### Genuine strengths

The evidence chain is candid. The support-baseline spike retired the plan's own earlier benchmark when a query-free prototype beat the model (22.83 against 21.22). The aspect spike reported a flat result (SE 11.32 against CLIP 11.13, with the interval spanning 0). Both results were allowed to change the problem. The held ledger with scripts that refuse a second run, the fresh-seed GO test and the cross-fitted fusion weights for baselines show a design that tries not to fool itself. My challenges concern what the design can and cannot distinguish. They do not question its honesty.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

A sceptic would say the evaluated task is a closed-inventory switch, not example-conditioned similarity. Each dataset has two or three aspects, and the plan builds one training pseudo-partition per evaluation aspect, on purpose: GoEmotions clusters for emotion, image k-means for style (adjusted mutual information 0.32 with style), caption-content k-means for genre, and on CUB per-sentence caption clusters chosen because Reed's captions describe parts such as the crown or the beak. The GoEmotions classifier is itself supervised and names 6 of the 8 test emotions. A model trained this way can carve one subspace per known aspect, and the four support and four contrast pairs then only have to signal which of two or three ready-made subspaces to use. Under that reading, a gain on ArtELingo reflects distant supervision of the evaluation aspects plus a K-way selector. The statement that the aspect is fixed at test time only by examples stays true of the interface while saying nothing about whether the model can infer a respect of similarity it was not trained on. No cross-modal dataset in the plan holds an aspect out of the training partitions, so both readings predict the same tables. The appended evidence does not favour the thesis either. On value episodes a query-free prototype beat the factor model. On aspect episodes every example-based scorer sat within 0.3 points of CLIP, while privileged names gained 1.5 points pooled and 3.2 on emotion, the subjective aspect on which the examples-beat-names story depends. C3's asymmetry is largely what ArtEmis labelling produces: each row's emotion belongs to the annotator who wrote that caption, while about five annotators, who need not agree, share one image. If the method claim falls, what remains is K1 and a protocol that the plan's own novelty check calls a combination of existing parts.

### Challenges by claim

**The aspect inventory and C2 (table row C1).** R-pseudo names the risk that the model learns the episode format, and the plan says the cross-dataset and unseen-species results are the test. Neither result can test it. The 50 unseen CUB species keep the same aspects (colour, bill shape) and the same values. Every other dataset gets its own aspect-matched partitions (§6 table). GeneCIS, where COCO-trained factors use generic image and caption k-means, is the one place where the training partitions do not mirror the evaluation aspects. But GeneCIS is image to image, its example protocol is declared non-comparable, and K5 has no decision rule. So the claim that the learned basis is "the prior that makes it possible to estimate a similarity from just four example pairs" cannot be refuted as planned. A minimal repair is a leave-one-aspect-out run on ArtELingo, for instance training without the caption-content partition and testing genre episodes, and generic partitions on CUB that are not tuned to attribute groups. Each needs a decision rule fixed in advance against the raw metric-from-pairs baseline. A stronger option is a partition bank larger than the evaluation aspects, built without looking at them.

**C3 (table row M1).** The backbone test cannot separate two explanations that both predict a flat weak-side probe. One is that images carry little emotion. The other is that a per-annotation emotion label is only partly determined by the image, because several annotators label the same painting and need not agree. The plan has 308,723 rows over 61,402 paintings, about five annotations per painting, and each caption was written to explain its own row's label. So the caption probe partly reads the label's author, while the image probe faces the spread among about five annotators. The aspect spike and the backbone check both record this label noise as a caveat. The C3 wording and the backbone verdict still draw the stronger conclusion about what pixels hold. The cap statement is also close to true by construction, because any cross-modal aspect match must read the aspect on both sides. The repair is to report the image-side emotion probe and ceiling against painting-level soft or majority labels, with inter-annotator agreement as the bound. Unless the corrected analysis still shows the gap, C3 should be stated as a property of the annotation protocol: ArtEmis captions are elicited as emotion explanations, Reed captions as descriptions. Separately, §5.3 asserts that genre is "the one ArtELingo aspect visible in both modalities" without a probe, although C3 relies on genre as its within-dataset control.

**K2, K6 and K7 against §10 (table row M2).** K2 promises wins over unsupervised bases, in both directions, on every dataset. §10 makes only three comparisons primary, on the metric pooled over directions, and declares every other comparison descriptive. K6 (the rule selects aspect factors, and the factors are shared) and K7 (the gain comes from the learned basis) have no decision rule at all. The aspect spike already showed that swap success reaches about 50% for any antisymmetric scorer at chance R@1, so swap analysis alone cannot carry K6. Pooling also means that a method gaining only on genre, the aspect expected to be readable in both modalities, would pass both the GO and the primary comparisons, while emotion, the paper's lead example, has no protection. The repair is to promote the K2 and K7 comparisons and a per-aspect rule (at least for emotion) to pre-declared status with a multiplicity plan, or to reword K2, K6 and K7 to what a descriptive analysis supports.

**K3 (table row M3).** "Matches" is undefined. §10 defines "beats" as a CI lower bound above 0 but sets no equivalence margin, and the support spike already reads an interval covering 0 as matched. Under that reading, "match it on objective ones" holds whenever the test is underpowered. Which aspects count as subjective is also undefined, and R-names commits in advance to narrowing K3 to where names fail, so the claim is built to survive any outcome. The appended evidence points the other way: privileged names gained 3.2 points on emotion and nothing on style, while every example-based scorer was flat. The prepared answer in the literature review cites CLAY's 4.9 mAP gain on mood, which shows that names are weak on mood. It does not show that examples are strong. The repair is to define subjective and objective aspects before E10, set an equivalence margin for "matches", and state which result would falsify K3.

**K5 (table row M4).** K5 claims competitiveness with the published frozen B/32 rows on GeneCIS focus attribute. But §5.4 lists four-task averages as the comparators (SEARLE 14.4, CIReVL 15.9, OSrCIR 17.4). The focus-attribute column of the same rows in literature review Table 2 reads 18.9, 17.9 and 19.4. The plan's bar is therefore 2 to 4.5 points too low. Beyond that, the example protocol is declared non-comparable, the comparable text protocol is a stretch goal, and "competitive" has no threshold. GeneCIS is image to image, so it cannot test the cross-modal claim in any case. The repair is to use the focus-attribute column, define "competitive", and either commit to the text protocol or drop the comparison with published rows from K5.

**C2 attribution (table row M5).** C2 credits the shared, sparse, non-negative structure. Every planned control either has no training signal (PCA, NMF, SpLiCE, raw features) or is fit on eight pairs, while the method trains on pseudo-aspect episodes over 183,694 scorer-train rows. A K7 win therefore cannot separate structure from training signal. Three controls are missing. The first uses the same pseudo-partitions without the factor structure: per-partition probes trained on scorer-train rows and scored by the same agreement rule. For emotion this is the GoEmotions teacher distilled into an image probe, the cross-modal teacher that §8 rules out on the premise that GoEmotions cannot read images. The second is a dense, unconstrained projection trained on the same episodes. The third is the split-dictionary ablation that the literature review's prepared answer promises but that appears in neither §8 nor E11.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. A K-way aspect selector trained with distant supervision explains any ArtELingo gain at least as simply as example-conditioned similarity (row C1).
2. The modality asymmetry of C3 may be an artefact of the annotation protocol and per-annotation labels, not a property of modalities (row M1).
3. A distilled-teacher path: probes trained to predict the pseudo-partition clusters from each modality, combined by the agreement rule, would be a cheap competitor to method A. The plan excludes it on a false premise (row M5).
4. An in-context MLLM reranker given the same example pairs is the most direct example-conditioned competitor, yet it is only a stretch item. Without it, the question of whether a 2026 VLM given the examples already solves the task stays open, and none of the three primary comparisons is an example-conditioned model other than per-episode metrics.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- The ArtEmis and ArtELingo annotators, whose individual and often diverging responses define the emotion target.
- Human judges of aspect similarity: no step checks that people would pick the designated target as "similar in emotion".
- Speakers of the non-English ArtELingo languages, since only the English part is used.
- The users who would supply the examples, such as curators or retrieval users, for whom 4 + 4 cross-item pairs may cost more than naming the aspect.

### Unexamined Premise

The plan treats similarity in a respect as exact equality of one categorical label on that aspect. An episode's target is the candidate with the anchor's emotion class or style class, and every other candidate counts as equally wrong. That turns the task into cross-modal label-value matching, which favours label-supervised probes and partitions shaped like the labels. It leaves out graded closeness (fear nearer to awe than to contentment) and paintings that evoke several emotions. The motivating scenario, a user asking for things "similar in the emotion they convey", is therefore evaluated through a classification-transfer proxy whose fit to the scenario is never tested.

### Minor issues

- The GO test reuses the selection rows with fresh seed-43 episodes. This reduces, but does not remove, the winner's curse from picking the best of about ten runs, because luck tied to particular rows carries over. The statement that this "does not inflate the result" is too strong.
- The bootstrap resamples anchors, while held paintings recur across anchors, candidates and example pairs. A bootstrap clustered by painting would be more conservative. This is marked [FIELD-NORM UNVERIFIED], since CV retrieval papers rarely report clustered intervals, and it is kept minor.
- K1 says value episodes are "solved by the supports alone". The evidence shows that a query-free prototype (22.83) beats the factor model and that the query adds 0.57. It does not show that the task is solved.
- The CUB third aspect is chosen by the highest min(image, caption) probe gain, which selects a symmetric aspect. Using CUB as C3's symmetric control then partly reflects that selection.
- "Swap success is reported at matched R@1" does not say how R@1 is matched.
- The GO reference of 11.1 for backbone-only comes from the two-aspect spike. The E0 module adds genre, so the reference should be recomputed on the new episodes.

### Observations (Non-Defects)

- The fallback to a task and analysis paper is written down before any method result exists, and it is tied to a dated decision. That limits post hoc reframing of the storyline.
- The reuse of the ArtELingo held rows is disclosed, and the plan commits to saying so in the paper.
- The KISSME objection is anticipated with a closed-form baseline family and a named risk (R-kissme).
