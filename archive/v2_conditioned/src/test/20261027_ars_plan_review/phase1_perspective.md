## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor) is mandatory and belongs to the methodology seat. It asks whether the study design, the data handling, the statistics and the affordances for reproduction reach the bar a peer reviewer in this field would set. Because the contract describes the manuscript as a research plan written before its results, I read this dimension as asking whether the planned experiments, baselines, held-out splits and tests are able to confirm or disconfirm each stated claim, not whether results already exist. My seat does not score it. It matters to me only because a similarity claim borrowed from another discipline is no more testable than the protocol meant to test it.

D2 (domain_accuracy) is mandatory and belongs to the domain seat. It asks that claims agree with the current evidence of the field, that earlier work is described faithfully, and that field-specific terms and reported results contain no factual errors. My seat does not score it. It borders on my concern where terms imported from the psychology of similarity or from affect research are used, but in this panel that check sits under D4.

D3 (argumentative_coherence) is mandatory and is scored by the devil's advocate and methodology seats. It asks that the central thesis hold together, that the evidence offered actually carries the conclusions drawn from it, and that no fallacy undermines the main argument. The contract adds that the appended evidence, negative results included, should be weighed for how likely each claim is. My seat does not score it.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance) is high priority and is the only dimension my seat scores. It asks whether a reader from an adjacent field can follow the framing, the definitions and the stated implications, and whether any claim that reaches into another discipline is backed by evidence. From the psychology of similarity, "similar" is never a free-standing relation: things are similar in some respect, and the weight each feature receives shifts with context and task (Goodman's objection, Tversky's contrast model and its diagnosticity effect, the respects-for-similarity account of Medin, Goldstone and Gentner). I therefore read D4 as asking whether the plan states, in terms a cognitive scientist or an annotation researcher would recognise, what a condition or aspect is, where its ground truth comes from, how far human raters agree on that ground truth, and whether the planned design could test any claim that a learned representation mirrors a human notion. Following the stage note, an untested claim is acceptable at the plan stage only when the design could test it; a claim the design leaves untestable is what counts against this dimension.

D5 (writing_and_structure) is normal priority and belongs to the editor-in-chief seat. It asks how the document is organised, how clearly it explains itself, how good its figures and tables are, and whether it follows the conventions of its venue. My seat does not score it, although a definition that an outside reader cannot find in the text also weakens D4.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution) is mandatory and belongs to the editor-in-chief seat. It asks whether the work suits the target venue and offers an original and significant contribution for that venue's readers. My seat does not score it, and since no review criteria binding was supplied, I make no claim about fit to any venue.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance

dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: An operational definition of conditional similarity and of each conditioning aspect that an adjacent-field reader can map onto known constructs (similarity in a respect, context-dependent feature weighting as in Tversky's contrast model, attribute versus relational similarity as in structure-mapping); for every aspect whose ground truth comes from human judgment, the label source, the number of raters per item, the reported inter-rater agreement and the noise ceiling it implies; a planned held-out test that could separate a genuinely aspect-conditioned similarity from proxies such as label co-occurrence, caption wording or a single dominant factor; implications beyond the retrieval benchmark worded no more strongly than the planned design and the appended evidence, negative results included, can bear; adjacent-field terms used in their established sense; missing results by themselves do not count against this dimension.
what_triggers_block: A claim that a learned or conditioned similarity matches a human notion of similarity or a human-judged attribute is left untestable by the planned design, because no planned evaluation compares against independent human judgments and the only evidence is the same labels used in training; or a central term such as condition, aspect or conditional similarity has no operational definition, so an adjacent-field reader cannot tell what is being claimed.
what_triggers_warn: Definitions exist and the core claims are testable, but the framing is under-connected for adjacent readers: established constructs from the psychology of similarity or from affect research are missing or misused; human-derived aspect labels serve as ground truth without reported rater agreement or a noise ceiling; or implications for perception, art or human similarity judgment are worded more strongly than the planned evidence or the appended negative results support.
