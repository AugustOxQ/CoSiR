FCMI makes a clustering blind to a grouping we already know; for us that grouping would be content

12 October 2026, for idea (iii) of the step-2 discussion. Sources: the FCMI paper (Zeng et al., CVPR 2023; main text only, its supplement not read); our literature note 2026-11-28_design_l_novelty.md; round 7's report 2026-11-26_design_l_style_source.md.

Summary

  • FCMI is a deep clustering method from "fair clustering". It is given a known grouping of the data and builds clusters that are independent of it.
  • We would give it our content grouping as the known grouping. Its clusters would then have to follow something other than content, we hope style.
  • Our literature note names it, with one older method, as the closest prior work for "a grouping that cuts across content".
  • Round 7 suggests a caution: the true styles are not independent of content either, so full independence may also push away from style.
  • The choice for this idea is yours: a grouping builder, a comparator, or drop it for now.

How we got here

Our method steers an image–caption score by one of a few groupings of the paintings. A learned reader picks the grouping from the example pairs. One of the groupings should follow style. Today it comes from CSD, a style encoder, but CSD groups paintings by genre and content about as much as by style.

Round 7 ("design L") tried to build a better style grouping without labels, with the content part removed. No candidate passed the test on development data. Some removal methods kept genre (the graph cuts). Others removed style together with genre (the projections).

On 12 October we discussed four ideas for the next grouping. Idea (ii) is a small learned head on frozen CLIP features (CLIP is our image–text backbone), trained so that content cannot separate the pairs. FCMI is idea (iii).

1. The problem FCMI solves

Problem. A clustering follows the strongest structure in the data. Sometimes that structure is one we do not want. For example, a plain clustering of digits from two data sets (MNIST and USPS) may split by data set, not by digit.

Idea. The FCMI paper calls the unwanted grouping the protected attribute, a term from fairness work (gender, race, a data source). A clustering is fair when every cluster holds the protected groups in the same proportions as the whole data set. Then the clusters cannot follow the protected grouping, so they must follow something else.

In the paper's single-cell test, the protected attribute is the lab technique used to measure cells; as we read it, the clusters should follow the cell type. Its digit test reads, to us, as data set protected and digit as the target. Its main text does not name the setup of its other four tests.

Fairness alone is easy and useless: put everything in one cluster. So FCMI also asks for clusters that are compact and balanced in size, and for features that keep the data's information.

2. How FCMI works

FCMI trains a small auto-encoder with a clustering head. Below, X is the input, C the cluster and G the protected group. "Mutual information" I(·;·) means how much knowing one thing tells you about another; it is zero when the two are independent.

Part of the lossWhat it doesGoal
Clustering: raise I(X; C | G) Each item commits to one cluster, and the clusters have similar sizes Compact, balanced clusters
Fairness: lower I(G; C) The cluster says nothing about the protected group; zero exactly when every cluster holds the global proportions Clusters independent of the known grouping
Reconstruction The decoder rebuilds each input; it has one branch per protected group, and the item's group picks the branch Features keep their information

The per-group decoder branch has a purpose. The fairness term pushes group information out of the clusters. The decoder gets the group back from its branch, so the hidden features do not have to drop it.

On two of its six data sets, the paper's input is precomputed ResNet-50 features, not raw images. We have not checked whether its code takes our feature vectors.

FCMI paper: Section 3 (the loss) and the experiment setup. Code: the paper points to its authors' page; our literature note lists a public repository.

3. How we would use it

Idea. Run FCMI with the roles turned around:

  • Input: one vector per painting, from CSD or from CLIP.
  • Protected group: the painting's content group, from our 64-group CLIP image grouping (the "content grouping").
  • Output: a new grouping in which every group mixes all content groups. It would replace the CSD style grouping among the reader's four groupings.

In the two FCMI tests we could read, what is protected is a nuisance: the data source or the lab technique. Ours is the inverse: content is protected, and the target is style. Our literature note found no work that does this on frozen CLIP features or on art. That is "to our knowledge", from a limited search, at medium-low confidence.

Your generality principle. It says: encoders fixed in advance and chosen without the data set's aspect labels; the grouping step knows nothing of aspects. FCMI fits if its number of clusters and its fairness weight are also fixed in advance, or picked by episode R@1 as in round 7. Tuning them against the style labels would break it.

4. What round 7 suggests about it

The caution. Full fairness asks the new groups to share nothing with content. But the true style labels themselves share something with content. The table shows how much each grouping agrees with the true styles and with the content grouping. The measure is AMI: 0 is chance, 1 is identical. These numbers are descriptive.

GroupingAgrees with true stylesAgrees with content grouping
True style labels1.000.32
Raw CSD0.340.40
CSD, graph cut (V3)0.330.38
CSD, graph cut (V2)0.320.33
CSD, content projected out (V1)0.100.11
  • The true styles agree with the content grouping at 0.32. So a grouping with zero agreement with content cannot match the true styles fully.
  • The graph cuts lowered content a little (from 0.40 to 0.38 and 0.33) and kept style.
  • The projection lowered content a lot (to 0.11), and style fell with it (to 0.10).
  • No round 7 grouping reached low agreement with content while it kept style.

The projection is a different tool from FCMI: it removes linear content directions and is not a fair clustering. So this is a caution, not a test of FCMI. FCMI aims at full independence. Its fairness weight could aim lower. The FCMI paper reports results that are stable across its two weights on its digit test, so we do not know how much that setting changes. Our reading: only a partial setting could keep style, if any.

What the input brings. FCMI can only regroup what its input carries. On CSD, it would inherit the mix of style and genre that you suspected. On CLIP, groupings of CLIP image features already agree with the true styles to some degree: 0.32 for the 64-group content grouping, and 0.28 in round 7's step-3 notes. Their group counts differ from raw CSD's, so compare them with its 0.34 only loosely. So CLIP carries some style, mixed with content. Our reading: whether FCMI can pull the two apart is open.

How it relates to idea (ii). Both ignore content. Idea (ii) does it when it learns the features, with negatives from the same content group. FCMI does it when it forms the clusters, with an independence target. Both face the caution above.

5. Cost and role

  • Code: public. We have not checked its input format; we estimate a day or two to make it take our vectors.
  • Compute: a small auto-encoder on about 36,500 painting vectors. We estimate minutes to an hour on the local GPU.
  • Role in a paper: our literature note names FCMI and Cui et al.'s orthogonal re-clustering (2007) as the closest prior work for this step. It lists FCMI among the cheap label-free comparators for the grouping step. Its must-run comparators are other methods: X-Cluster (idea (iv)) and a method that names the aspect, then retrieves with it.
Our view (open after you form yours)

As our main grouping builder, FCMI is the idea that the round 7 caution hits hardest. It aims at full independence from content, and the true styles are not independent of content.

As a comparator, it is useful. Suppose idea (ii) goes ahead. Then FCMI on the same input is a natural comparator at the grouping step: the same goal, reached when the clusters are formed.

So we would keep (iii) as a comparator in the same loop as (ii), not as a line of its own. This is our view; the choice is yours.

Glossary

  • Fair clustering: clustering whose clusters each hold a known grouping's groups in the global proportions.
  • Protected attribute (protected group): the known grouping that the clusters must not follow.
  • Mutual information: how much knowing one variable tells you about another; zero when they are independent.
  • Content grouping: our 64-group k-means grouping of CLIP image features; one of the reader's groupings.
  • AMI: adjusted mutual information between two groupings; 0 is chance, 1 is identical.
  • CSD: the style encoder behind round 7's groupings. CLIP: our frozen image–text backbone.
  • V1, V2, V3: round 7's groupings: content projected out of CSD (V1), and two graph cuts of CSD across the content grouping (V2, V3).
  • Idea (ii): a learned grouping head on frozen CLIP, trained with negatives from the query's own content group.
