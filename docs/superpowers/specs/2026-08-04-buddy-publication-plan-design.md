## 1. Goal

Turn ~2 months of "conditional buddies" work (a cross-modal mutual-kNN graph used to give each sample's trainable condition vector a content-aware geometric initialization) into a submission-ready paper within **1–2 months**, at the highest venue tier the evidence honestly supports.

This spec covers: the venue/framing decision, the exact set of new experiments needed (in priority order, with success criteria and decision rules), the statistical standards to hold them to, and the timeline. It does **not** re-derive anything already validated — see §2 for what is already load-bearing.

Of the three properties Exp. 9 was designed to test, caption diversity and visual homogeneity remain genuinely null under both Pearson and rank (Spearman) correlation. Size is different: it clears a moderate rank/log-transformed correlation (Spearman ρ=−0.523; a linear Pearson of −0.328 understates it because the relationship is curved, not straight-line) — but a direct purity check (obs_s/deg_s, recoverable from `subreddit_lift`'s own internals) shows this is **not** a content effect: purity actually *rises* with size (ρ=+0.214), the opposite of what a "large subreddits are topically diluted" story would predict. The real cause is structural — a subreddit's edge-endpoint degree `deg_s` tracks its size almost exactly (ρ=+0.973), and the lift formula's own expected-count baseline scales with `deg_s`, mechanically pulling lift toward `1/size` regardless of content. So this is a legitimate null result for caption diversity and visual homogeneity, and a real-but-structural (not content-driven) finding for size: once the lift metric's own size-driven normalization is accounted for, none of the three candidate properties explains residual signal-strength variation. Neither reading touches the validated claim that the signal itself is real and strong on average, and it says nothing new about C4–C6's downstream-use questions. **Same-day extension:** re-run at two more independently-drawn RedCaps scales (300k, 500k, `build_subsample.py`/`extract_features.py` generalized to produce them — not Experiment 1's same-named prefix-sliced training stores, which are subreddit-degenerate) reproduces every sign and rough magnitude above, with subreddit coverage rising monotonically (159→197→214 of 350) as N grows — evidence the picture is stable, not a 150k-specific artifact. A new companion significance metric (enrichment z-score) independently corroborates the size finding's structural (not content-driven) origin: unlike lift, z correlates *positively* with size, as expected from a count-based confidence statistic. 1M/3.1M scales remain unexplored, deliberately deferred pending this confirmation. **C7 (Exp. 10) directly targets C6's still-open gap-to-CLIP question and comes back null**: a diagnostic-confirmed graph-construction flaw (98% of buddy-graph edges diluted toward statistical noise by the fixed-alpha blend) was fixed with an opt-in `distance_mode="typed"`, but the fix changes neither `test_oracle` retrieval nor `test_pre_diff`'s gap to CLIP by more than noise (all four deltas within the ~0.1–0.7 R1 floor, none clearing `mean/SEM ≥ 2`) — it does not newly establish that the gap to CLIP is unfixable in general, only that this specific, previously-undiagnosed construction flaw was not the (or not a measurable) cause of it at RedCaps-150k. **C8 (Exp. 8) tests whether the buddy graph's specific source encoder pair matters, and answers Experiment 6's deferred survival-vs-usefulness question along the way**: across all 16 of C3's cross-VLM pairs on RedCaps-150k, t2i is a clean null (all pairs within/near noise of the CLIP-sourced default) but i2t is a real, seed-replicated positive result — 12 of 15 non-baseline pairs clear the significance bar against the current default, and 10 of those 12 also clear the ~0.1–0.7 R1 noise floor in magnitude (the other 2, `clip_img:e5` and `vit_sup:bge`, are statistically flagged but noise-floor-marginal, the same standard applied to t2i's `dinov2:minilm`), several substantially, meaning the CLIP-sourced graph is actually one of the *worse* choices for i2t among those tested; joining each pair's delta against its C3 survival rate finds no relationship for t2i (r=+0.01) and a moderate negative relationship for i2t (r=−0.41), which substantially answers Experiment 6's question (no for t2i, weakly-negative for i2t) and is why Experiment 6 is dropped from scope (§6/§8) — it does not newly establish that a non-CLIP-sourced graph is the better choice in general, only that it is at RedCaps-150k, this one operating point, for the 16 pairs tested; nor does the i2t survival-rate correlation establish a causal mechanism, only a correlational (r=−0.41, n=15) relationship. **C10 (Exp. 12/12.2/12.3 + same-day `combine_side` robustness check) narrows Exp. 12's original "behaviorally inert" framing rather than confirming or overturning it wholesale**: the ~80%-bridge-node false-transitivity structure Exp. 12 found does leave a small, real fingerprint on trained-vs-frozen retrieval rank, but it is concentrated in one node subgroup (`img_only_only`) and that concentration itself turns out to be a `combine_side` artifact — it relocates cleanly to `txt_only_only` nodes when the condition is fused into the text side instead. The much larger i2t-vs-t2i asymmetry that C9 established is architecturally distinct: it survives the same `combine_side` swap (i2t stays the significant direction), just at roughly 1/11th the magnitude — so `combine_side` amplifies but does not solely cause it, and what does remains an open, unexplained question for a future experiment, not answered here.

## 3. Venue & framing decision

### 3.1 Assessment

The project's methodological rigor (seed replication, paired significance, analytic nulls, an explicit confound quantified and traced to its cause) is well above the bar for most venues. The gap is not quality — it's that **no experiment yet shows the buddy signal produces a retrieval win over the untouched CLIP baseline**, which is the load-bearing claim a "novel initialization method" paper needs. Exp. 1 (C5, C6) now shows buddy-init beats the generic `imgtxt` alternative — a real, seed-replicated, within-model win — but at RedCaps-300k (the one scale where the comparison has actually been checked, C6) both initializations still retrieve worse than raw frozen CLIP on the same test set; buddy-init only narrows that gap (59–73%), it doesn't close it. So the load-bearing claim as originally scoped — beat CLIP, not just beat a weaker alternative init — remains open.

### 3.3 Framing decision (approved)

**Primary spine: rigorous analysis/robustness paper.** *"A content-adjacency graph signal in image–text data is real and encoder-agnostic — but exploiting it as auxiliary training supervision is a cautionary tale: only one of three natural mechanisms shows any effect, and that effect is a measured near-duplicate confound, not transferable signal."* This framing is defensible **regardless of Exp. 1's outcome** — if Exp. 1 is positive, it upgrades from "signal validated, training-use mostly doesn't work" to "signal validated AND useful at init, training-use mostly doesn't help further" (strictly stronger, same spine). If Exp. 1 is negative, the paper still stands on C1–C4 plus the closed loop of "we checked whether the validated signal is useful anywhere, including the most basic use, and report exactly what we found."

This decouples the *submission decision* from experimental risk — the stretch tier is upside, not a requirement.

**Resolved (Exp. 1 ran, C5):** the actual outcome was neither cleanly positive nor negative — a real, seed-replicated, direction-and-dataset-dependent effect (RedCaps: clean win both directions; Impressions: wins i2t, loses t2i). The framing survives this exactly as designed: it upgrades from "signal validated, training-use mostly doesn't work" to "signal validated, and *the better of two initializers* where the signal is cleanest (RedCaps); on the dataset most affected by the same near-duplicate structure C4 already flagged (Impressions), the initializer trades t2i for i2t rather than helping outright" — strictly more interesting than either the pure-positive or pure-negative branch anticipated here, and still the same spine. See `docs/reports/2026-08-16_buddy_init_ablation.md` for the full result and §2's C5 row.

**Revised after C6 (2026-08-19):** "useful as an initializer" above overstates it. At RedCaps-300k, the one scale where this was actually checked against the untouched CLIP baseline, neither `imgtxt` nor `buddies` beats raw CLIP retrieval — buddy-init only narrows the gap `imgtxt` leaves open (59–73%, depending on direction). The honest spine is now: *"the graph signal is real (C1–C3), robust (C3), and — of the two initializers tested — buddy-graph structure is the better one (C5/C6); but the conditioning approach this project builds around that signal has not yet been shown to retrieve better than doing nothing (plain frozen CLIP) at any scale checked."* This is still a defensible, TMLR-shaped analysis/robustness paper — arguably a cleaner and more honest one — but it is a materially different (weaker) claim than "useful as an initializer" implied, and should be the framing used going forward, not the pre-C6 language above. See `docs/reports/2026-08-19_buddy_init_ablation_redcaps_300k.md` §"Buddy-init vs. raw CLIP" and §2's C6 row.

### 3.4 Prior-art risk (checked — Experiment 0; verdict yellow, stress-tested twice)

Experiment 0 ran (`docs/reports/2026-08-10_prior_art_note.md`, updated 2026-08-11 and again 2026-08-15 with two rounds of deeper adversarial follow-up passes). Verdict: **yellow, not red** — related-but-distinguishable prior art exists and must be cited, but no fatal overlap was found across either pass.

The closest construction-level match is **CMFG** (*"Mutual contextual relation-guided dynamic graph networks for cross-modal image-text retrieval,"* Scientific Reports 2025, PMC12484662), which builds a cross-modal mutual-kNN graph via the same construction primitive as conditional-buddies (independent per-modality k-NN, then mutually-linked across modalities). A second pass, searching a completely unrelated field (bilingual lexicon induction), found the same primitive again in **Artetxe 2018** (ACL, aclanthology.org/P18-1073) and **MUSE** (Conneau et al., ICLR 2018, arXiv:1710.04087) — mutual nearest neighbors found between two independently-trained monolingual embedding spaces, used to seed an iterative self-learning dictionary. This means the paper's related-work section **cannot claim the graph-construction mechanism itself is unprecedented** — that framing is now known to be false in two independent fields and would be a citable gap a literate reviewer catches immediately. Both CMFG and Artetxe/MUSE diverge from conditional-buddies entirely on *use*: CMFG keeps the graph alive as a continuous GCN-message-passing mechanism over trainable encoders every forward pass; Artetxe/MUSE re-invoke the mutual-NN step every refinement iteration. Neither is ever a one-shot initializer over a frozen backbone.

The novelty claim therefore narrows and sharpens rather than disappears: not "nobody builds this graph" but "nobody uses it as a one-shot initializer of a frozen-backbone, per-sample trainable vector, independently stress-tested by rebuilding the graph from held-out encoders never used to build it (6 held-out encoders, 16 cross-VLM pairs)." Also required in related work: NNCLR (nearest-neighbor positives in SSL), mean-shift/prototype-based SSL (SwAV, MSF — same continuous-training-signal category as CMFG), and LEPORID (graph-Laplacian-to-init precedent, but single-domain, not cross-modal). A broader sweep of CV retrieval/clustering/hashing/zero-shot-init lineages (k-reciprocal re-ranking, consensus-driven face clustering (CDP), graph-regularized cross-modal hashing, zero-shot GCN class-embedding init) turned up nothing closer than the above; CDP (Zhan et al., ECCV 2018) is worth a defensive citation specifically for the held-out-encoder validation methodology, since its committee-of-models graph-agreement check is the closest located analogue to that check even though its downstream use (continuous pseudo-labeling) is unrelated. C4's near-duplicate-confound finding (Family #2's apparent win on Impressions is 40.6%/279×-enriched same-photo edges, does not replicate on RedCaps) remains a legitimate, citable counterpoint to the entire neighbor-as-continuous-signal family (NNCLR, MSF, CMFG) — framed as *quantifying* a concern the field already generally knows about (DINOv2 runs dedup as data curation), not discovering it from scratch.

Net effect on this spec: no venue-tier change, but the related-work section is now a **required, non-trivial five-way citation-and-differentiation job** (CMFG, LEPORID, NNCLR, MSF/SwAV, Artetxe 2018 + MUSE as a paired citation), plus CDP as a defensive citation for the validation methodology — not an optional positioning paragraph. That this list grew under a second, deliberately broader adversarial pass (rather than shrinking or staying put) and the init-only/frozen-backbone/cross-encoder-robustness axis still held every time is itself the strongest evidence yet that this axis, not the graph-construction mechanism, is the right novelty claim to carry into the writing timeline (§6).

## 4. Experiment plan

All experiments reuse existing infrastructure (feature caching, buddy-init templates, `analyze_buddy_families.py`-style paired analysis, the seed-replication pattern already used throughout `docs/reports/2026-06-24_buddy_progress_report.md` §8). None require new model architecture or new encoders.

**Statistical standard for every new experiment (§5 governs in detail):** ≥3 seeds, paired-within-seed Δ, report mean ± std and mean/SEM, using the same significance convention already used in the progress report (`mean/SEM` roughly ≥ 2 read as significant; compare to the measured noise floor of ~0.1–0.7 R1 from a duplicate-config run, not to zero).

Produce a short internal note: what's been done before, what's genuinely different here (candidate answer: per-sample *trainable condition vector initialization* in a frozen-CLIP + gated-combiner architecture, validated via encoder-robustness rather than proposed as a new SSL training method), and whether the C4 confound-diagnosis result reframes as a useful counterpoint to NNCLR-style neighbor-as-positive claims.

- **Success criteria:** Not pass/fail. Deliverable is the internal note itself, feeding directly into the paper's eventual related-work section. ### Experiment 1 — Buddy-init-only vs. imgtxt-init-only (critical path, do first)

- **What:** `initialization_strategy ∈ {imgtxt, buddies}`, all training-time terms off (`lambda_buddy=0`, `lambda_buddy_con=0`, `buddy_refresh=False` — i.e. the existing baseline arm of every family sweep, split by init strategy instead of held fixed at `buddies`), 3 seeds, on Impressions and RedCaps (COCO if Exp. 5 is in scope by then).
- **Why:** This is the paper's foundational claim and is currently unmeasured anywhere in the repo (confirmed by grep across `docs/`, `.claude/`, `scripts/` — every existing sweep fixes `initialization_strategy=buddies` and varies only loss terms; see `scripts/run_buddyreg_full.sh:49` etc.). Every other family-ablation number in the progress report is a delta *from* buddy-init, not a comparison *to* the prior init.
- **Tooling:** New sweep script mirroring `scripts/run_buddy_seeds.sh`'s pattern (fixed operating point, seed as the replication axis) but with `initialization_strategy` as the swept axis instead of a lambda; new analysis mode in `analyze_buddy_families.py` (or a sibling script) keyed on init strategy.

  - **Negative** (imgtxt beats buddies) → same reframe as null, with an added discussion point (worth investigating briefly, not a blocking sub-study).
- **Cost:** ~2×3×2 = 12 runs (2 init strategies × 3 seeds × 2 datasets), reusing the standard 250-epoch Impressions / 100-epoch RedCaps schedules already established. Cheapest and highest-priority item in this plan.

### Experiment 8 — Buddy-init encoder-pair ablation (added 2026-08-24; stretch, gated on 1–4 landing on schedule)

Sweep/analysis outputs (per-pair buddy-init tensors, results tables) live in a new dated folder, `src/test/20260824_buddy_init_encoder_ablation/`, per the repo's existing debugging-folder convention — kept separate from the raw feature caches below.
- **Feature storage:** The 150k pass reuses the existing `heldout_feats/<dataset>/<model>.npy` cache as-is (`src/test/20260708_heldout_grid/extract_heldout.py`, single source of truth via its `cache_path()` function, already imported by `cross_vlm_buddy.py`) — untouched, since C3's report already depends on those exact paths. This keeps the cache easy to locate (two fixed directories), reusable (one function, no hardcoded paths), and safely deletable (`heldout_feats_300k/` is self-contained and nothing else depends on it, unlike the 150k dir — `rm -rf` it once Exp. 8's 300k report is written).

CLIP backbone, combiner, and all training-time buddy terms fixed throughout — only the init-source encoder pair varies. Build and validate the pipeline at 150k first (matches C5's scale, reuses existing feature cache, no extraction cost); extend the same sweep to 300k afterward, since that is the scale where a CLIP-baseline comparison is meaningful (C6) — this extension additionally requires re-running held-out feature extraction for all 6 encoders at 300k before the sweep itself can start.

If all 16 cluster near the current CLIP-sourced result, that is itself evidence that the *graph structure* — not the specific encoder — is what drives usefulness, strengthening C2/C3's "not an encoder artifact" claim from a new (downstream-use) angle rather than only the signal-validation angle those claims currently rest on.

A further 48 runs at 300k if the 150k pass is informative enough to justify the extension, plus a one-time 300k re-extraction pass (6 encoders × 1 dataset) as a prerequisite. Largest single compute item added to this plan — gated the same way as Experiments 5/7 (pursued only if 1–4 land on schedule).

### Experiment 9 — What predicts buddy-signal strength across RedCaps subreddits? (cheap, independent — run anytime)

- **What:** `subreddit_lift()` (`src/test/20260623_redcaps_buddy/redcaps_buddy.py`) already computes per-subreddit lift but only reports the top-15 most-enriched. Extend it to every subreddit with enough samples, then correlate per-subreddit lift against three subreddit-level properties: size (sample count), caption diversity (mean pairwise text-embedding distance, or vocabulary size, within the subreddit), and visual homogeneity (mean pairwise CLIP image-embedding distance within the subreddit).
- **Why:** C1's validation story currently rests on one aggregate ~20× lift number for RedCaps. A per-subreddit breakdown explained by measurable properties (e.g., "signal is strongest in visually homogeneous, caption-diverse subreddits") upgrades C1 from "the signal exists on average" to a mechanistic account of *when* it's strong — more citable, and more interesting to a TMLR reviewer, than a single global number. This deepens the signal-validation claim (C1); it is not a re-measurement of the downstream training-use question (C5/C6).
- **Tooling:** Reuses existing `subreddit_of`, `subreddit_lift`, and cached RedCaps CLIP features — no new graph-building infrastructure. New one-shot analysis script producing a per-subreddit table plus a correlation/regression against the three properties, and a scatter figure.
- **Success criteria:** Not pass/fail — a deliverable table/figure regardless of outcome. A clean correlation with one or more properties is a positive addition to C1's narrative; a null correlation is still useful (rules out these three properties as confounds/explanations of signal strength).
- **Cost:** Cheap — no training, no new feature extraction. Days, not compute; independent of every other experiment in this plan, so it can run in parallel with anything, including Experiment 0.

### Experiment 10 — Modality-provenance-aware distance mixing (typed edges) (added 2026-08-24; stretch, gated on a cheap diagnostic that already ran positive)

- **What:** `compute_buddy_init` builds the union buddy graph `E = A_img ∪ A_txt`, then computes both an image-cosine-distance and a text-cosine-distance for *every* edge of `E` and blends them with a fixed `alpha*D_img + (1-alpha)*D_txt`, regardless of which modality(ies) actually support that edge (`mix_distances`, `src/conditional_buddy/buddy_graph.py`). A no-training diagnostic (`src/test/20260824_buddy_graph_disagreement/diagnose_disagreement.py`, run on real RedCaps 150k and 300k data, both scales agreeing) found that **only ~1.6–1.9% of edges are cross-modally confirmed ("both")** — the other **~98%** are single-modality-only (57% img-only, 42% txt-only) — and for those edges, the fixed blend collapses a genuinely good supporting-modality rank (median 0.22–0.30, where 0 = best) down to **median 0.50–0.51 — statistically indistinguishable from a random pair** — while ~80% of nodes are "bridge" nodes connecting to different neighbors via different, non-overlapping modalities (the structural precondition for spectral false-transitivity). This experiment adds an opt-in `distance_mode: "typed"` (default stays `"blend"`, the current behavior, fully unaffected) that uses each edge's own supporting modality's rank alone for img-only/txt-only edges, keeping the blend only for `both` (no disagreement to correct) and `repair` edges (added by `ensure_min_degree`/`ensure_connected`, owned by neither modality) — then runs the same paired-ablation pattern as Experiments 1/8 to test whether this changes retrieval, and specifically whether it narrows C6's still-open gap to raw CLIP (`test_pre_diff`, already logged automatically by every eval call in `src/eval/pipeline.py` — no new eval code needed).
- **Why:** A literature review (multi-view/multiplex graph fusion: MVN2VEC, HAN, R-GCN, co-regularized/conflicting-views spectral clustering — MvKDR, CI-GMVC) found the general principle "preserve per-view provenance rather than blending disagreeing views into one weight" well-established elsewhere, but confirmed this project's exact compound failure (union of two per-modality mutual-kNN graphs + fixed global blend + resulting bridge-node risk, specifically for a one-shot spectral training initializer) is not directly addressed in prior work — see the diagnostic's own module docstring for the full citation trail. Given C6's central open finding — buddy-init narrows but does not close the retrieval gap to raw CLIP — a construction step that is empirically shown to collapse ~98% of the graph's edges to near-random before the spectral step ever runs is a concrete, previously-undiagnosed, and cheaply-fixable candidate explanation, not a speculative one.
- **Tooling:** New `distance_mode` parameter threaded through `compute_buddy_init`/`mix_distances` (or a sibling function), reusing the diagnostic's own edge-classification logic (`classify_edges`, already tested against synthetic cases including a hand-verified dilution direction). New `train.buddies.distance_mode` config key, added to the buddies template-compatibility `_extra` dict in `train_cosir.py` (same integration pattern as Experiment 8's `encoder_pair` and the existing `b_weight`) so a stale template from one mode is never silently reused under the other. New sweep script mirroring `scripts/run_init_ablation.sh`/`run_buddy_init_encoder_ablation.sh` (`distance_mode ∈ {blend, typed}` as the template-key axis, 3-seed Hydra multirun inner loop), and a new analysis script reading `test_oracle/*` (retrieval) and `test_raw`/`test_pre_diff` (already-instrumented CLIP-baseline comparison, per C6's precedent) from wandb.
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k only (matches C5's scale, cheapest iteration), same fixed operating point as C5/C6 (`lr=1e-3, lr_label=1e-4, embedding_dim=16, alpha=0.5`), `initialization_strategy=buddies` fixed, all training-time buddy terms off (isolating the init-construction effect, same discipline as Experiment 1). 2 arms (`blend`, `typed`) × 3 seeds = 6 runs. Does not touch the frozen CLIP training backbone (init-construction-only change) — same non-violation of §9's backbone boundary as Experiment 8, and not a new training-time loss mechanism (§9's "no Family #4" boundary applies to loss terms during training, not to how the init graph's edge distances are computed).
- **Success criteria:** **Positive** (`typed` beats `blend` on `test_oracle` retrieval, seed-replicated, mean/SEM ≥ 2, AND narrows the `test_pre_diff` gap to CLIP) → a genuine, previously-undiagnosed methodological improvement, worth its own paragraph/ablation table in the paper and a candidate to become the new default. **Null** (no reliable retrieval difference) → the dilution is real (diagnostic-confirmed) but doesn't matter for the downstream spectral embedding's usefulness — still a legitimate, citable negative result (a graph-construction flaw that doesn't propagate to a measurable training effect). **Negative** (`typed` is worse) → investigate briefly (e.g., whether the diluted blend was accidentally acting as a regularizer), but not a blocking finding either way — report as found.
- **Cost:** 6 runs at 150k (~100-epoch schedule) — same shape and cost as the cheapest cell of Experiment 1. The diagnostic itself (already run, no additional cost) is what justifies spending this compute rather than treating the finding as diagnostic-only.

### Experiment 11 — Does post-init training of the conditions do anything? (added 2026-08-25)

**11.1 — Frozen vs. trained conditions (do first; gates 11.2)**

- **What:** `initialization_strategy=buddies` (unchanged), two arms: (a) **trained** — today's default, conditions update every step under the full existing loss stack; (b) **frozen** — conditions fixed at their buddy-init value for the whole run, only the combiner (and other model params) train. Pure config toggle, no new code: the EM-alternation mechanism already in `train_cosir.py` (`train.em_interval`) sets `embedding_manager.embeddings.requires_grad_(False)` whenever `epoch // em_interval` is even ("network" phase); setting `em_interval` to any value ≥ `epochs` keeps it in that phase for the entire run, with `_prev_em_phase` triggering the freeze at epoch 0. Every other hyperparameter (all `loss.lambda_*`, `oracle_guided`, lr, lr_label, dim=16, alpha=0.5) is held identical between arms — isolating exactly one variable: whether gradient reaches the condition table at all after init.
- **Why:** Directly tests whether the current default's post-init training pressure on conditions earns its keep, or whether the buddy-init geometry alone is doing the useful work. No existing experiment (C1–C8) tests this — C4 tested a *specific, explicit* training-time use of the buddy graph (contrastive supervision directly on conditions) and found it a confound-driven null; this asks the more basic question about the *implicit* pressure every default run already applies (in-batch InfoNCE via the combiner, plus the VICReg/laplacian/oracle-guided machinery described in §2's framing discussion above), independent of whether buddy structure is invoked during training at all.
- **Tooling:** New sweep script mirroring `run_buddy_init_encoder_ablation.sh`'s pattern (single-flag axis, seed as the Hydra multirun axis) — `em_interval ∈ {-1, <sentinel≥epochs>}` as the swept axis. Reuses the existing `_z_init` drift logging (`train_cosir.py`, already computed every run — mean `‖z − z_init‖` at eval cadence — but never reported) to show, as free context, how far the trained arm's conditions actually moved from init and whether that correlates with the retrieval delta.
  - **New embedding-geometry diagnostic** (`scripts/analyze_condition_geometry.py`, reusable beyond Exp. 11): retrieval numbers alone can miss a real difference between arms, so this runs unconditionally on both arms' checkpoints, not gated on 11.1's retrieval verdict. The per-epoch caching this needs already exists and requires no training-side changes — `_save_condition_viz_snapshot` (`train_cosir.py`) unconditionally writes `condition_viz/epoch_XXXX.pt` (full per-sample condition table `label_embeddings_all` + `combiner_state_dict`/`predictor_state_dict`/`other_proj_state_dict` at that epoch) and `condition_viz/fixed_data.pt` (frozen CLIP embeddings) on every eval epoch. For each saved epoch, the script rebuilds `Combiner_new` from the saved state dict, pulls the frozen train-set **combine-side** features from `FeatureManager`'s cache reindexed into condition-table order via the existing `reorder_features_to_z` (`src/metrics/regularizer.py` — reused specifically to avoid the sample-ID mismatch class of bug CLAUDE.md flags as this repo's most common), and computes `comb_emb = combine(combine_side_feat, condition)` for the train set. (The combine side is whichever modality the run's `model.combine_side` names — **`img` for all 6 of Exp. 11.1's runs**, so "combine-side feature" means the frozen CLIP *image* feature there. The features must be fed **raw / un-normalized**, exactly as `train_cosir.py` and `src/eval/metrics.py` do, because `Combiner_new.forward` is a scale-sensitive gated residual; the first implementation normalized them and produced physically-impossible geometry numbers — see the report's Method section.) It reports: (1) distribution comparison of conditioned vs. unconditioned embedding geometry (pairwise-similarity spread, PCA effective dims — reusing `compute_condition_space_quality`'s existing-but-currently-disabled logic from `condition_space_evaluator.py`); (2) per-sample shift `1 − cos(comb_emb_i, combine_side_feat_i)`, its distribution, and correlation against condition norm, buddy-graph degree, and sample type; (3) most/least-changed sample ranking by shift, with IDs/captions for qualitative spot-check; (4) a condition-vs-combine-side cross grid: sample a small set of combine-side features and a small set of conditions, compute `comb[i,j] = combine(feat_i, condition_j)` for every pair, then split the diversity two ways — for each combine-side feature, diversity *across conditions* (low ⇒ conditions are interchangeable/null for that input, the combiner is ignoring the condition), and for each condition, diversity *across combine-side features* (low ⇒ that condition collapses every input to nearly the same output, dominating the combination) — the two collapse failure modes retrieval numbers alone cannot distinguish from each other; (5) all of the above across every saved epoch, so it shows a trajectory — including for the frozen arm, where conditions are fixed but the combiner still trains, so `comb_emb` (and the grid diagnostic) can still shift.
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k, matched operating point (lr=1e-3, lr_label=1e-4, dim=16, alpha=0.5), all training-time buddy-graph terms off (same discipline as Exp. 1/8/10 — this experiment is about the *default* implicit pressure, not the buddy-graph families). 3 seeds × 2 arms = 6 runs.
- **Success criteria:** **No real difference on either axis** (retrieval within noise floor AND the geometry diagnostic shows no meaningful divergence in shift distribution/ranking between arms) → the current training-time pressure on conditions is not earning its complexity; buddy-init geometry alone does the work — a clean simplification result, no 11.2 needed. **A real difference on either axis** — retrieval (seed-replicated, mean/SEM ≥ 2, exceeding the noise floor, either direction) **or** geometry (a clear divergence in the shift distribution, most/least-changed ranking, condition-space quality metrics, or the condition-vs-combine-side grid diversity split between arms even if retrieval is a null) → proceed to 11.2. A geometry-only divergence with null retrieval is itself a reportable finding (training reshapes the space without moving the retrieval numbers) and is exactly the case retrieval-only analysis would have missed.
- **Cost:** 6 runs, ~100-epoch RedCaps schedule — same shape as Experiment 10, the cheapest paired ablation in this plan. The geometry diagnostic is post-hoc/CPU-cheap (no additional training), run once per saved checkpoint after the 6 runs complete.

**11.2 — Deeper diagnosis (gated on 11.1 finding a real difference on either the retrieval or the geometry axis)**

- **What:** Contingent on 11.1's outcome, one of two diagnostic branches (exact grid to be scoped once 11.1's result is known, not committed up front):
  - *If trained beats frozen (on retrieval, geometry, or both):* ablate the loss-stack terms that actually reach the condition table's gradient one at a time against the frozen baseline (`lambda_laplacian`, `lambda_collapse`, `lambda_var`/`lambda_cov`, `oracle_guided` on/off) to find which term(s) are responsible for the win — same single-lever-at-a-time discipline as every other experiment in this plan. Also a natural point to test the currently-disabled `lambda_gap_align` (condition-pairwise similarity ≈ text–image gap-pairwise similarity) as a candidate *replacement* for the implicit pressure, since it is the one existing term with an explicit, content-grounded target. The geometry diagnostic from 11.1 reruns on each ablated arm's checkpoints to see which term actually moves the embedding geometry, not just the retrieval number.
  - *If frozen beats trained, the result is mixed, or the divergence is geometry-only (retrieval null):* extend the 11.1 geometry diagnostic rather than launch new training — correlate per-sample condition drift (`‖z_i − z_init,i‖`, already loggable from 11.1's trained-arm checkpoints) and per-sample embedding shift against per-sample retrieval outcome, to characterize *what* the training pressure is doing (e.g., do the samples that drift/shift most also regress most, cluster by buddy-graph degree, or look orthogonal to the retrieval effect entirely).
- **Why:** 11.1 answers *whether* post-init training matters, on two independent axes; 11.2 answers *why*, which is the actionable finding for the paper (either "here is the one term worth keeping, drop the rest" or "here is what's going wrong geometrically, and why buddy-init alone is preferable even though retrieval alone wouldn't have shown it"). Note: this reuses only already-implemented loss terms (including `lambda_gap_align`, which exists in the loss config but is disabled by default) and the 11.1 geometry script — it does not introduce a new training-time mechanism, so it does not violate §9's "no Family #4" boundary.

Per 11.2's own branching above ("if trained beats frozen... ablate the loss-stack terms" vs. "if frozen beats trained, the result is mixed, or the divergence is geometry-only: extend the geometry diagnostic rather than launch new training"), the actual result — frozen beats trained on i2t, t2i null, and the geometry divergence is directional (trained shifts further and its conditions matter more) but is not a retrieval win for trained on any axis — is the spec's **second branch by its literal wording** (it is at minimum "the result is mixed"): extend the 11.1 geometry diagnostic (correlate per-sample condition drift and per-sample embedding shift against per-sample retrieval outcome) rather than launch a new loss-stack-term ablation sweep. This branch reuses 11.1's own checkpoints/`condition_geometry/summary.json` files at near-zero additional compute, no new training runs. 11.2 has since run on all 3 real seed pairs (`scripts/analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py --pair`, `n_query_sample=3000`, no new training) and the result is **not** a null. The *naive* signed cross-arm delta (trained run's rank minus frozen run's rank) does come back near-zero for both `condition_drift` (rho = +0.026/+0.017/+0.009, p = 0.16–0.60) and `embedding_shift` (rho = +0.046/+0.040/+0.046, p < 0.03) — but that estimator differences two *independently-trained models* (separate combiners, separate `other_proj`, separate condition tables; std ≈ 1055–1343 ranks), so the condition table's own contribution is swamped by whole-model divergence. With a **condition-only counterfactual** — the trained arm's combiner/`other_proj`/gallery held fixed, only the condition table swapped trained→buddy-init — the condition's own effect on per-sample rank is large and tightly seed-replicated: rho(`delta_rank_swap`, `condition_drift`) = **+0.466/+0.466/+0.477**, p < 1e-160, 3/3 seeds, in the mechanically expected direction (high-drift samples lose the most when reverted to init). Neither mismatch is fixable by re-running (per-sample conditions exist only for training samples). So 11.2 establishes that the condition table's per-sample retrieval effect is real, large and drift-graded, but it **constrains rather than resolves** 11.1's held-out oracle regression; closing that gap would require a held-out-reachable diagnostic (i.e. `condition_predictor`), outside this branch's no-new-training scope. See `docs/reports/2026-08-25_condition_freeze_ablation.md`'s "Experiment 11.2" section for the full per-seed results, qualitative extremes, and interpretation.
- **Cost:** Not yet estimated — depends on which branch and how many terms are in play; expect the same order of magnitude as 11.1 (single-digit-to-low-teens runs) if the ablate-loss-terms branch is taken, near-zero additional compute if the drift/geometry-correlation branch is taken (reuses 11.1's checkpoints and script).

**11.3 — Bidirectional table↔predictor coupling (independent of 11.1/11.2's gate; extends this plan's scope per explicit 2026-08-26 sign-off — see §9 amendment)**

- **What:** `condition_predictor` (`src/model/condition_predictor.py`) is trained today via one-way distillation: `pred_loss = (1 - F.cosine_similarity(pred_cond, label_embeddings.detach(), dim=-1)).mean()` (`train_cosir.py`, condition-predictor-distillation block, `lambda_pred=1.0` by default) — gradient flows table→predictor only, never predictor→table, because of the `.detach()`. This experiment removes that `.detach()`, gated by a new config flag `loss.pred_stopgrad` (default `True` = today's behavior, `False` = new arm), so the existing `lambda_pred` weight now also pulls the condition table toward what the predictor (a bounded-capacity MLP over the frozen combine-side feature) can represent. One new arm (`pred_stopgrad=False`) × 3 seeds = 3 runs; only meaningful under the trained-condition regime — a no-op under the frozen arm since the table has `requires_grad=False` there, so no new frozen-arm runs are needed.
- **Why:** 11.1's headline `test_oracle` metric never touches `condition_predictor` at all — `compute_oracle_recall_average` (`src/eval/metrics.py`) tries every entry in the training table against each held-out query and takes the max ("oracle-max-over-conditions"), so the frozen-beats-trained gap it found is about whether the *trained table itself*, as a codebook, contains entries that generalize to novel queries — not about predictor quality. Today nothing pressures individual table entries to stay representable by a smooth function of their own input feature; 11.1 already showed trained conditions drift further from init than frozen ones (`shift_mean` Δ = +0.0081, mean/SEM = +13.9, 3/3 seeds) and 11.2 showed that drift is real and drives large in-sample rank changes (rho = +0.466/+0.466/+0.477 vs. drift under the condition-only counterfactual, 3/3 seeds) but never resolved whether that drift helps or hurts the held-out codebook. Removing the detach adds an explicit consistency force pulling each table entry back toward the "feature-predictable" manifold buddy-init already occupies (the same manifold a kNN-in-feature-space estimate lives on), while the entry retains its own direct InfoNCE gradient — a falsifiable bet that the table can land on a *richer* point on that manifold than raw buddy-init averaging, not merely converge back to it. Not guaranteed: if the coupling is too weak it will look identical to today's trained arm (informative null); if it's unstable, table and predictor could chase each other into worse drift than today's uncoupled trained arm (an equally reportable failure mode, not just the positive case).
- **Tooling:** One new config flag (`loss.pred_stopgrad: bool = True`) and a one-line conditional around the existing `.detach()` call — no other code changes. Both primary metrics are already computed by the exact eval call 11.1 uses: `test_oracle` and `test_pre_diff`/`test_pre_original` (`compute_predictor_recall`, a single forward pass through `condition_predictor`, minus the raw unconditioned CLIP baseline) are both produced unconditionally by `TestEvaluator.evaluate(..., use_oracle=True)` whenever `hasattr(model, "condition_predictor")` (`src/eval/pipeline.py:305-351`) — no new eval code, no separate pass. `_z_init` drift magnitude and the `pred_loss` trajectory are also already logged and reused as free diagnostic context.
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k, matched operating point (lr=1e-3, lr_label=1e-4, dim=16, alpha=0.5), identical to 11.1/11.2. 1 new arm × 3 seeds = 3 runs.
- **Success criteria (all three directions stated up front, per this plan's own discipline):** `test_oracle` and/or `test_pre_diff` (i2t/t2i R1) seed-replicated (3/3) and above the established noise floor, recovering toward or past the frozen arm → real signal; escalate to a `lambda_pred` strength sweep (its current value of 1.0 was tuned for one-directional distillation, so may be miscalibrated for a bidirectional pull), gated on this result exactly as 11.2 was gated on 11.1. Both metrics flat vs. today's (uncoupled) trained arm → clean, cheap null — the coupling doesn't reach the failure mode, mechanism ruled out at minimal cost. Either metric *worse* than today's trained arm → reportable instability (table↔predictor coupling amplifies rather than dampens drift), reported regardless of direction, not cherry-picked.
- **Cost:** 3 runs, same order of magnitude as one arm of 11.1 — the cheapest item in this plan.

### Experiment 12 — Cross-modal polysemy: bridge-node structure and a false-transitivity audit (added 2026-08-26; cheap, independent — run anytime)

- **What:** `classify_edges()`/`bridge_node_stats()` (currently a one-off diagnostic in `src/test/20260824_buddy_graph_disagreement/diagnose_disagreement.py`) already found that **~80% of RedCaps-150k nodes are "bridge" nodes** — connected to at least one neighbor *solely* via the image modality's mutual-kNN graph and at least one neighbor *solely* via the text modality's. That is structurally exactly the pattern this experiment names directly: a sample A whose image is close to B's in vision-embedding space but whose text is close to C's in text-embedding space, i.e. a candidate for polysemy (A admits multiple valid cross-modal interpretations). This experiment (a) promotes `classify_edges`/`bridge_node_stats` from the one-off diagnostic into `src/conditional_buddy/buddy_graph.py` as reusable public functions (unit-tested, same synthetic-graph style as the diagnostic's own existing tests); (b) derives a **per-node polysemy label** — `img_only_only` / `txt_only_only` / `bridge` / `neither` — directly from each node's own edge composition, no new graph construction; (c) for a subsampled set of bridge nodes (capped the way 11.2 capped `n_query_sample=3000`), extracts (B, C) pairs — B an img-only neighbor, C a txt-only neighbor of the same bridge node — and measures whether the existing buddy-init embedding places B and C *closer together* than a degree-matched random-pair baseline, and whether that pull (if any) is **graded** by a second-order-proximity score (2-step random-walk transition probability, or shared-neighbor Jaccard) or is flat/arbitrary; (d) correlates the per-node polysemy label against per-sample retrieval-rank and `condition_drift` values from 11.2's own pipeline (`analyze_pair()` in `scripts/analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py` already computes `delta_rank`/`drift_query`/`shift_trained` per query sample internally — today it only persists their aggregates/extremes to `condition_geometry/retrieval_correlation_vs_frozen.json`, not the full per-sample arrays, so this experiment needs one small, in-scope extension: an opt-in flag on `analyze_pair()` that additionally dumps the full per-sample arrays it already computes, keyed by `query_sample_ids`, to a sibling `.npz` — reusing 11.2's exact computation, not re-deriving it).
- **Why:** Song & Soleymani 2019 ("Polysemous Visual-Semantic Embedding", CVPR) and Alomari et al. 2026 ("Lenses: Toward Polysemous Vision-Language Understanding", CVPR) establish polysemy-as-multiple-valid-interpretations as a recognized phenomenon in vision-language representation, typically addressed by giving each sample multiple embedding slots. This experiment asks a prior, much cheaper question first: does this project's existing *single*-embedding buddy-graph construction already implicitly reflect that structure (via which modality supports which edge), and does its spectral-embedding step handle the resulting multi-hop relation between B and C in a graded, principled way — per the account in Coifman & Lafon 2006 (diffusion maps: why shared-neighbor structure appears in eigenvector embeddings, with diffusion-time controlling how much weight it gets) and the first-order/second-order proximity distinction in Tang et al. 2015 (LINE) and Cao et al. 2015 (GraRep) — or whether it manifests as the "false transitivity" Experiment 10's own diagnostic module docstring flagged as a risk but never actually measured. This is squarely diagnostic, not corrective: Experiment 10 already showed that even when edge-type (modality provenance) is known and used (`distance_mode="typed"`), it doesn't move downstream retrieval at this scale, because the current one-scalar-per-edge spectral pipeline is provably near-invariant to that information under a global rescale. Experiment 12 asks a different, unaddressed question — not "does typing edges change the embedding" but "does the graph already encode a meaningful B–C relation at all, or is any apparent closeness noise." A null (B–C indistinguishable from a matched random pair) is a clean, citable reinforcement of the "buddy-init geometry alone is fine" throughline running through 11.1–11.3. A positive that is graded by real shared-neighbor structure is a legitimate emergent property worth naming in the paper. A positive that is *ungraded* (uniform/arbitrary regardless of shared-neighbor overlap) confirms Experiment 10's flagged risk as an actual distortion — a genuine, previously-unmeasured limitation, and the trigger (per this plan's own gating discipline, same as 11.1→11.2 and 11.3's own escalation rule) for scoping a future modality-aware representation change (e.g. Liang et al. 2020/2022's consistent/inconsistent graph-component decomposition, or R-GCN-style typed-edge message passing) as its own, separately-approved experiment — not committed here.
- **Tooling:** No new training infrastructure. New analysis script `scripts/analyze_polysemy_bridges.py` (sibling to `analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py`), operating on: (i) `A_img`/`A_txt`/`E` rebuilt via the existing `build_buddy_graphs` (CPU-cheap at 150k, seconds) or loaded from a saved `buddy_edges.npy`; (ii) one already-completed buddy-init template's `embeddings.npy` (e.g. from an 11.1 run, same graph/config as C5); (iii) a new per-sample `.npz` dump added to `analyze_pair()` in `scripts/analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py` (query_sample_ids/delta_rank/condition_drift/embedding_shift, computed there already, just not persisted today), joined by sample id. `classify_edges`/`bridge_node_stats` move into `src/conditional_buddy/buddy_graph.py` with their existing test coverage carried over and extended with a `both`-edge sanity baseline (should show ~zero B–C pull, no disagreement expected there — the same sanity-baseline pattern the original diagnostic already used for `both` edges).
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k only, same operating point as Experiments 9–11 (K=30, α=0.5). Reuses one already-completed buddy-init template and 11.2's saved outputs — zero new training runs, zero new feature extraction.
- **Success criteria (three-way, stated up front per this plan's own discipline):** **Real + graded** — B–C embedded distance significantly closer than the degree-matched baseline, and that gap correlates with the second-order-proximity score → legitimate emergent structure, a citable positive finding, and the trigger for scoping a future modality-aware representation experiment (not committed here). **Real + ungraded** — B–C pulled closer but the pull does not correlate with second-order proximity (flat/arbitrary) → confirms Experiment 10's flagged "false transitivity" risk as an actual distortion; a citable cautionary/limitations finding, no new mechanism required to report it. **Null** — B–C statistically indistinguishable from the baseline → reinforces "buddy-init geometry alone is already fine," folding into 11.1–11.3's cumulative throughline. Independently of the bridge-pair result, the per-node polysemy label vs. 11.2's retrieval-rank/drift correlation is reported regardless of outcome (same "report both directions" discipline as every other experiment in this plan).
- **Cost:** No new training or feature extraction — one new analysis script plus promoting two existing functions (with unit tests) out of a one-off diagnostic into the main package. Cheapest item in this plan alongside Experiment 9; independent of every other experiment, can run anytime.

- **What:** Contingent on 12.2 finding a late-onset (not flat-from-start) gap, and on confirming that intermediate `condition_viz/epoch_*.pt` snapshots exist for all 9 runs (11.1's own geometry diagnostic already reads these per-epoch snapshots for its 6 runs, but this is not yet confirmed for 11.3's 3 `pred_coupled` runs specifically): repeat 12.3's per-sample bridge cross-reference at an early epoch (e.g. 10 or 20) and a mid epoch (e.g. 50), not just the final epoch, for at least the seed-1 `trained`-vs-`frozen` pair, to see whether any bridge-vs-`delta_rank` effect is confined to the period where `trained` is actually diverging from `frozen` (per 12.2), or is present uniformly regardless of that divergence.
- **Why:** Further localizes when/whether a bridge effect emerges, tying 12.3's cross-recipe replication directly to 12.2's trajectory finding — the natural third leg once the first two land, not a standalone commitment.
- **Tooling:** Reuses the same per-sample dump/cross-reference machinery as 12.3, pointed at earlier `condition_viz/epoch_*.pt` snapshots instead of the final checkpoint (needs `scripts/analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py` to accept an epoch selector if it does not already, a small scoped extension).
- **Scope:** Reuses existing checkpoints if intermediate snapshots exist — zero new training. Not committed: whether this task runs at all depends on 12.2's result and on confirming snapshot availability, decided after 12.2/12.3 land, not up front.
- **Success criteria:** Not yet defined precisely — to be scoped once 12.2/12.3's actual results are known, per this plan's own discipline of not pre-committing to an untriggered branch's exact grid.
- **Cost:** Unknown until snapshot availability and 12.2's shape are confirmed; likely cheap (reuse) if snapshots exist, otherwise deferred or dropped.

**12.5 — `combine_side` disambiguating replication (added 2026-08-27, ad hoc — raised in conversation, not pre-planned)**

- **What:** All of 11.x/12.x's runs use the config default `model.combine_side="img"` — the trainable condition is fused (via `CombinerGated`) into the image embedding only; the text embedding passes through `other_proj`, an identity-initialized `nn.Linear` (`src/model/cosirmodel.py`). This raised a direct hypothesis: is 12.3's `img_only_only`-concentrated bridge effect, and/or C9's i2t-vs-t2i asymmetry itself, actually an artifact of always combining on the image side, rather than a property of the buddy graph or the training dynamics per se? This task re-ran the full 11.1 freeze-ablation + 12.3 bridge cross-reference pipeline with a one-line Hydra override, `model.combine_side=txt` (`EXTRA_OVERRIDES` env var already supported by `scripts/run_condition_freeze_ablation.sh`), 3 seeds × 2 arms (trained/frozen), same RedCaps-150k operating point as every other 11.x/12.x run.
- **Why:** A clean architectural confound in every prior 11.x/12.x result — `combine_side` was never varied — that could fully or partially explain two of this plan's headline findings (C9's asymmetry, C10's subgroup concentration) via a mechanism (which modality gets the trainable condition) unrelated to the buddy graph's actual topology. Before Codex audits or further diagnostics were run on the *existing* combine_side="img" data, this was flagged as the cheapest, most direct disambiguating test: literally flip the architectural choice and see which findings move.
- **Tooling:** No new code — reused `run_condition_freeze_ablation.sh`'s existing `EXTRA_OVERRIDES`/`SEED_SWEEP`/`WANDB_GROUP` env vars, and the existing `analyze_condition_freeze_ablation.py` / `analyze_training_trajectory.py` / `analyze_condition_retrieval_correlation.py --dump-per-sample` / `analyze_polysemy_bridges.py` pipeline unchanged (pointed at the new `combine_side="txt"` run directories and wandb group instead of the default). A prior Codex-assisted audit (`docs/superpowers/scratch/2026-08-27_codex_combine_side_review.md`, not committed) had separately verified that every code path this pipeline touches (training snapshots, `test_oracle`, condition-drift/embedding-shift diagnostics) is already correctly `combine_side`-aware; it also found one real but currently-dormant bug (three non-oracle recall helpers in `src/eval/metrics.py` always combine text against a raw/unprojected image gallery regardless of `combine_side`) — not reachable from any path this plan's results use, documented as a docstring caveat only (`d7a5f93`), left unfixed per explicit instruction.
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k, `model.combine_side=txt`, otherwise identical operating point to 11.1/12.3. 3 seeds × 2 arms = 6 new runs.
- **Result:** The two candidate explanations split cleanly. **(a) The bridge-subgroup concentration IS `combine_side`-driven**: under txt-combine it relocates from `img_only_only` to `txt_only_only` (median delta_rank −8/−10/−3 vs. img-combine's −17 to −28 on `img_only_only`; `img_only_only` itself drops to −1/−2/−2, near the `bridge` baseline). **(b) C9's i2t-vs-t2i asymmetry is NOT explained by `combine_side` alone**: i2t remains the significant, larger-magnitude direction under txt-combine too (mean Δ=+0.40 R1, mean/SEM=+4.0*, seeds +0.50/+0.50/+0.20) — not flipped to t2i (mean Δ=+0.13, mean/SEM=+0.3, n.s.) — just ~11× smaller than img-combine's +4.67/+32.1. So `combine_side="img"` amplifies but does not solely cause the asymmetry; what the remaining ~11× amplification factor's actual mechanism is remains an open, unexplained question.
- **Cost:** 6 runs, same shape as one arm-pair of 11.1 — cheap, reused the exact same sweep/analysis tooling. Full writeup: `docs/reports/2026-08-27_combine_side_txt_replication.md`.

## 5. Statistical methodology (standardize across all new experiments)

These are not new conventions — they're what the project already does in `2026-06-24_buddy_progress_report.md` §8 and `2026-07-16_buddy_cross_vlm_survival.md`; this section exists so every new experiment (1–4, 7) is held to the same bar as the existing ones, since a reviewer will read them side by side.

1. **≥3 seeds for every comparison that feeds a paper claim.** The single-seed grid in the original Family #1/#3 ablation was demonstrably misleading (see §8a of the progress report) — this is not a hypothetical risk, it already happened once in this project.
2. **Paired-within-seed reporting**: mean Δ ± std across seeds, plus `mean/SEM` as the significance read, consistent with the existing convention (e.g. `mean/SEM = 7.0`, `z = +11`).
3. **A measured noise floor, not a zero baseline.** The progress report established the noise floor (~0.1–0.7 R1) from a duplicate-config run; any new experiment claiming a real effect should be checked against that floor, not against Δ=0.
4. **Analytic nulls over Monte Carlo where a closed form exists** — the project already made this switch once (`7226e5b perf: replace Monte-Carlo permutation null with closed-form analytic null`); no new experiment in this plan needs a new null construction, but if one comes up, prefer the closed form.
5. **No silent cherry-picking of operating points.** Where a sweep is involved (Exp. 2, 3), report the full curve, not just the best cell — the progress report's own peak-finding (λ_con dose-response, §8b) is the template: show the rollover, not just the peak.
