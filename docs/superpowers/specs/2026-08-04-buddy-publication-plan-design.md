## 1. Goal

Turn ~2 months of "conditional buddies" work (a cross-modal mutual-kNN graph used to give each sample's trainable condition vector a content-aware geometric initialization) into a submission-ready paper within **1–2 months**, at the highest venue tier the evidence honestly supports.

This spec covers: the venue/framing decision, the exact set of new experiments needed (in priority order, with success criteria and decision rules), the statistical standards to hold them to, and the timeline. It does **not** re-derive anything already validated — see §2 for what is already load-bearing.

**C1–C3 are the strongest part of the paper as it stands: rigorous, multi-probe, multi-dataset, multi-encoder robustness evidence for a real signal.** C4 is a well-instrumented negative/nuanced result, not a weakness — but on its own it does not answer whether the signal is *useful*, only that one way of using it during training mostly isn't (and the one way that seemed to be, wasn't really). **C5 closes that gap with a direction-and-dataset-dependent answer**: buddy-init is a real, seed-replicated improvement over generic init on RedCaps and on Impressions' i2t direction, but a real, seed-replicated regression on Impressions' t2i direction — net positive (3 of 4 cells), not a clean win, and not explained here (see the report's caveats). **C6 adds a scale check on the strongest cell, and a load-bearing caveat that likely also applies to C5**: the RedCaps win over `imgtxt` is not a 150k-specific artifact — it holds, undiminished, at double the training data — but it is a win over a weak within-model baseline, not over the untouched CLIP backbone the whole conditioning approach is built on top of. Whether C5's 150k numbers show the same shortfall against CLIP is unverified (that comparison was not run in the 150k report and the underlying wandb data was not available to re-check retroactively — see the 300k report's caveat section), not confirmed absent. Impressions was not re-run at 300k scale either (out of scope given the reporting deadline that bounded this round), so C6 says nothing new about the Impressions t2i regression. **Net effect on the paper's central claim: Exp. 1 (C5+C6) demonstrates buddy-graph structure is a better initializer than the generic alternative within this training approach — it does not yet demonstrate that this training approach's conditioned retrieval beats the frozen CLIP baseline it starts from**, which is a stronger and different claim than the "retrieval win" framing in §3.1 was written to expect. **C1a (Exp. 9) deepens C1 with a per-subreddit breakdown of the same RedCaps lift**: the aggregate ~20× figure hides a 150-fold spread (4.4×–671× lift across 159 qualifying subreddits). Of the three properties Exp. 9 was designed to test, caption diversity and visual homogeneity remain genuinely null under both Pearson and rank (Spearman) correlation. Size is different: it clears a moderate rank/log-transformed correlation (Spearman ρ=−0.523; a linear Pearson of −0.328 understates it because the relationship is curved, not straight-line) — but a direct purity check (obs_s/deg_s, recoverable from `subreddit_lift`'s own internals) shows this is **not** a content effect: purity actually *rises* with size (ρ=+0.214), the opposite of what a "large subreddits are topically diluted" story would predict. The real cause is structural — a subreddit's edge-endpoint degree `deg_s` tracks its size almost exactly (ρ=+0.973), and the lift formula's own expected-count baseline scales with `deg_s`, mechanically pulling lift toward `1/size` regardless of content. So this is a legitimate null result for caption diversity and visual homogeneity, and a real-but-structural (not content-driven) finding for size: once the lift metric's own size-driven normalization is accounted for, none of the three candidate properties explains residual signal-strength variation. Neither reading touches the validated claim that the signal itself is real and strong on average, and it says nothing new about C4–C6's downstream-use questions. **Same-day extension:** re-run at two more independently-drawn RedCaps scales (300k, 500k, `build_subsample.py`/`extract_features.py` generalized to produce them — not Experiment 1's same-named prefix-sliced training stores, which are subreddit-degenerate) reproduces every sign and rough magnitude above, with subreddit coverage rising monotonically (159→197→214 of 350) as N grows — evidence the picture is stable, not a 150k-specific artifact. A new companion significance metric (enrichment z-score) independently corroborates the size finding's structural (not content-driven) origin: unlike lift, z correlates *positively* with size, as expected from a count-based confidence statistic. 1M/3.1M scales remain unexplored, deliberately deferred pending this confirmation.

## 3. Venue & framing decision

### 3.1 Assessment

The project's methodological rigor (seed replication, paired significance, analytic nulls, an explicit confound quantified and traced to its cause) is well above the bar for most venues. The gap is not quality — it's that **no experiment yet shows the buddy signal produces a retrieval win over the untouched CLIP baseline**, which is the load-bearing claim a "novel initialization method" paper needs. Exp. 1 (C5, C6) now shows buddy-init beats the generic `imgtxt` alternative — a real, seed-replicated, within-model win — but at RedCaps-300k (the one scale where the comparison has actually been checked, C6) both initializations still retrieve worse than raw frozen CLIP on the same test set; buddy-init only narrows that gap (59–73%), it doesn't close it. So the load-bearing claim as originally scoped — beat CLIP, not just beat a weaker alternative init — remains open.

### 3.3 Framing decision (approved)

**Primary spine: rigorous analysis/robustness paper.** *"A content-adjacency graph signal in image–text data is real and encoder-agnostic — but exploiting it as auxiliary training supervision is a cautionary tale: only one of three natural mechanisms shows any effect, and that effect is a measured near-duplicate confound, not transferable signal."* This framing is defensible **regardless of Exp. 1's outcome** — if Exp. 1 is positive, it upgrades from "signal validated, training-use mostly doesn't work" to "signal validated AND useful at init, training-use mostly doesn't help further" (strictly stronger, same spine). If Exp. 1 is negative, the paper still stands on C1–C4 plus the closed loop of "we checked whether the validated signal is useful anywhere, including the most basic use, and report exactly what we found."

This decouples the *submission decision* from experimental risk — the stretch tier is upside, not a requirement.

**Resolved (Exp. 1 ran, C5):** the actual outcome was neither cleanly positive nor negative — a real, seed-replicated, direction-and-dataset-dependent effect (RedCaps: clean win both directions; Impressions: wins i2t, loses t2i). The framing survives this exactly as designed: it upgrades from "signal validated, training-use mostly doesn't work" to "signal validated, and *the better of two initializers* where the signal is cleanest (RedCaps); on the dataset most affected by the same near-duplicate structure C4 already flagged (Impressions), the initializer trades t2i for i2t rather than helping outright" — strictly more interesting than either the pure-positive or pure-negative branch anticipated here, and still the same spine. See `docs/reports/2026-08-16_buddy_init_ablation.md` for the full result and §2's C5 row.

**Revised after C6 (2026-08-19):** "useful as an initializer" above overstates it. At RedCaps-300k, the one scale where this was actually checked against the untouched CLIP baseline, neither `imgtxt` nor `buddies` beats raw CLIP retrieval — buddy-init only narrows the gap `imgtxt` leaves open (59–73%, depending on direction). The honest spine is now: *"the graph signal is real (C1–C3), robust (C3), and — of the two initializers tested — buddy-graph structure is the better one (C5/C6); but the conditioning approach this project builds around that signal has not yet been shown to retrieve better than doing nothing (plain frozen CLIP) at any scale checked."* This is still a defensible, TMLR-shaped analysis/robustness paper — arguably a cleaner and more honest one — but it is a materially different (weaker) claim than "useful as an initializer" implied, and should be the framing used going forward, not the pre-C6 language above. See `docs/reports/2026-08-19_buddy_init_ablation_redcaps_300k.md` §"Buddy-init vs. raw CLIP" and §2's C6 row.

### 3.4 Prior-art risk (checked — Experiment 0; verdict yellow, stress-tested twice)

Experiment 0 ran (`docs/reports/2026-08-10_prior_art_note.md`, updated 2026-08-11 and again 2026-08-15 with two rounds of deeper adversarial follow-up passes). Verdict: **yellow, not red** — related-but-distinguishable prior art exists and must be cited, but no fatal overlap was found across either pass.

The closest construction-level match is **CMFG** (*"Mutual contextual relation-guided dynamic graph networks for cross-modal image-text retrieval,"* Scientific Reports 2025, PMC12484662), which builds a cross-modal mutual-kNN graph via the same construction primitive as conditional-buddies (independent per-modality k-NN, then mutually-linked across modalities). A second pass, searching a completely unrelated field (bilingual lexicon induction), found the same primitive again in **Artetxe 2018** (ACL, aclanthology.org/P18-1073) and **MUSE** (Conneau et al., ICLR 2018, arXiv:1710.04087) — mutual nearest neighbors found between two independently-trained monolingual embedding spaces, used to seed an iterative self-learning dictionary. This means the paper's related-work section **cannot claim the graph-construction mechanism itself is unprecedented** — that framing is now known to be false in two independent fields and would be a citable gap a literate reviewer catches immediately. Both CMFG and Artetxe/MUSE diverge from conditional-buddies entirely on *use*: CMFG keeps the graph alive as a continuous GCN-message-passing mechanism over trainable encoders every forward pass; Artetxe/MUSE re-invoke the mutual-NN step every refinement iteration. Neither is ever a one-shot initializer over a frozen backbone.

The novelty claim therefore narrows and sharpens rather than disappears: not "nobody builds this graph" but "nobody uses it as a one-shot initializer of a frozen-backbone, per-sample trainable vector, independently stress-tested by rebuilding the graph from held-out encoders never used to build it (6 held-out encoders, 16 cross-VLM pairs)." Also required in related work: NNCLR (nearest-neighbor positives in SSL), mean-shift/prototype-based SSL (SwAV, MSF — same continuous-training-signal category as CMFG), and LEPORID (graph-Laplacian-to-init precedent, but single-domain, not cross-modal). A broader sweep of CV retrieval/clustering/hashing/zero-shot-init lineages (k-reciprocal re-ranking, consensus-driven face clustering (CDP), graph-regularized cross-modal hashing, zero-shot GCN class-embedding init) turned up nothing closer than the above; CDP (Zhan et al., ECCV 2018) is worth a defensive citation specifically for the held-out-encoder validation methodology, since its committee-of-models graph-agreement check is the closest located analogue to that check even though its downstream use (continuous pseudo-labeling) is unrelated. C4's near-duplicate-confound finding (Family #2's apparent win on Impressions is 40.6%/279×-enriched same-photo edges, does not replicate on RedCaps) remains a legitimate, citable counterpoint to the entire neighbor-as-continuous-signal family (NNCLR, MSF, CMFG) — framed as *quantifying* a concern the field already generally knows about (DINOv2 runs dedup as data curation), not discovering it from scratch.

Net effect on this spec: no venue-tier change, but the related-work section is now a **required, non-trivial five-way citation-and-differentiation job** (CMFG, LEPORID, NNCLR, MSF/SwAV, Artetxe 2018 + MUSE as a paired citation), plus CDP as a defensive citation for the validation methodology — not an optional positioning paragraph. That this list grew under a second, deliberately broader adversarial pass (rather than shrinking or staying put) and the init-only/frozen-backbone/cross-encoder-robustness axis still held every time is itself the strongest evidence yet that this axis, not the graph-construction mechanism, is the right novelty claim to carry into the writing timeline (§6).

## 4. Experiment plan

All experiments reuse existing infrastructure (feature caching, buddy-init templates, `analyze_buddy_families.py`-style paired analysis, the seed-replication pattern already used throughout `docs/reports/2026-06-24_buddy_progress_report.md` §8). None require new model architecture or new encoders.

**Statistical standard for every new experiment (§5 governs in detail):** ≥3 seeds, paired-within-seed Δ, report mean ± std and mean/SEM, using the same significance convention already used in the progress report (`mean/SEM` roughly ≥ 2 read as significant; compare to the measured noise floor of ~0.1–0.7 R1 from a duplicate-config run, not to zero).

### Experiment 0 — Related-work grounding & prior-art differentiation check (do first, in parallel with Exp. 1)

- **What:** A focused literature pass (not an experiment — reading/writing only) against the neighbor/graph-based SSL lineage flagged in §3.4: NNCLR, mean-shift/prototype SSL (MSF, SwAV), and graph-Laplacian/spectral init precedent. Produce a short internal note: what's been done before, what's genuinely different here (candidate answer: per-sample *trainable condition vector initialization* in a frozen-CLIP + gated-combiner architecture, validated via encoder-robustness rather than proposed as a new SSL training method), and whether the C4 confound-diagnosis result reframes as a useful counterpoint to NNCLR-style neighbor-as-positive claims.
- **Why:** No related-work grounding exists anywhere in the project currently (confirmed by grep across `docs/`) despite it being scheduled only in weeks 6–8 (§6) as part of paper drafting. Discovering a fatal prior-art overlap during writing means the experiment budget is already spent; discovering it in week 1 means the framing (or, worst case, the venue tier) can still adapt.
- **Success criteria:** Not pass/fail. Deliverable is the internal note itself, feeding directly into the paper's eventual related-work section. A "red" outcome (a very close prior match with no clear differentiation) is itself decision-relevant — it would be grounds to revisit the TMLR framing before further compute is spent, not just before writing.
- **Cost:** Days, not compute. No infrastructure. Runs in parallel with Experiment 1, not sequentially before it.

### Experiment 1 — Buddy-init-only vs. imgtxt-init-only (critical path, do first)

- **What:** `initialization_strategy ∈ {imgtxt, buddies}`, all training-time terms off (`lambda_buddy=0`, `lambda_buddy_con=0`, `buddy_refresh=False` — i.e. the existing baseline arm of every family sweep, split by init strategy instead of held fixed at `buddies`), 3 seeds, on Impressions and RedCaps (COCO if Exp. 5 is in scope by then).
- **Why:** This is the paper's foundational claim and is currently unmeasured anywhere in the repo (confirmed by grep across `docs/`, `.claude/`, `scripts/` — every existing sweep fixes `initialization_strategy=buddies` and varies only loss terms; see `scripts/run_buddyreg_full.sh:49` etc.). Every other family-ablation number in the progress report is a delta *from* buddy-init, not a comparison *to* the prior init.
- **Tooling:** New sweep script mirroring `scripts/run_buddy_seeds.sh`'s pattern (fixed operating point, seed as the replication axis) but with `initialization_strategy` as the swept axis instead of a lambda; new analysis mode in `analyze_buddy_families.py` (or a sibling script) keyed on init strategy.
- **Success criteria / decision rule:**
  - **Positive** (buddy-init beats imgtxt-init, seed-replicated, mean/SEM ≥ 2, on ≥2/3 datasets) → paper leads with this as the headline positive result; stretch-tier venue becomes live (§3.2).
  - **Null** (no reliable difference) → paper's contribution reframes as "the signal is real and robust, but content-aware geometric initialization alone does not measurably improve retrieval over a generic PCA init" — still a legitimate, useful negative result, stays on the TMLR/workshop track.
  - **Negative** (imgtxt beats buddies) → same reframe as null, with an added discussion point (worth investigating briefly, not a blocking sub-study).
- **Cost:** ~2×3×2 = 12 runs (2 init strategies × 3 seeds × 2 datasets), reusing the standard 250-epoch Impressions / 100-epoch RedCaps schedules already established. Cheapest and highest-priority item in this plan.

### Experiment 2 — Gentler λ_con retune on RedCaps

- **What:** `lambda_buddy_con ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.3}` on RedCaps, 3 seeds. Already the field's own flagged next step (`2026-06-24_buddy_progress_report.md` §9): *"Decide #2's fate on clean data... one sweep decides it."*
- **Why:** Closes whether Family #2 (the one training-time mechanism with any measured effect) is net-positive at *some* dose off near-duplicates, or whether the mechanism itself doesn't transfer regardless of strength.
- **Tooling:** `LAMBDA_BUDDYCON_SWEEP=0,0.1,0.3 SEED_SWEEP=1,2,3 bash scripts/run_buddycon_redcaps.sh` (script already supports this override; confirmed at `scripts/run_buddycon_redcaps.sh:57`).
- **Success criteria:** Net-positive in both t2i and i2t directions at some dose → Family #2 survives as a real (if modest) contribution on clean data, strengthening C4 into a positive result at the right operating point. Null/negative at every dose → C4 stands as written; Family #2 is init-only-adjacent, not a training contribution.
- **Cost:** 3 doses × 3 seeds = 9 runs, RedCaps-scale (~100 epochs each, per the script's documented conservative schedule).

### Experiment 3 — B-lean init (`b_weight`) validated on RedCaps

- **What:** Sweep `train.buddies.b_weight ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8}` (the suggested grid from `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-08-b-lean-init-design.md`) on RedCaps, holding training-time terms off (init-geometry-only effect, per that spec's scope decision). 3 seeds.
- **Why:** The held-out grid (`2026-07-08_heldout_grid.md`) found strict-intersection graph B consistently cleaner than union E — but the progress report's §8d flags that B is 79.9% same-photo on Impressions, i.e. "B is cleaner" may itself be a near-duplicate artifact. This was never run on RedCaps, where B has *nothing* to lean on (82.5% of RedCaps samples have no strict buddy at all, per `2026-06-23_redcaps_buddy.md`).
- **Success criteria:** If β>1 shows no benefit (or harm) on RedCaps while showing benefit on Impressions, that's a second confirmed instance of the same near-duplicate-confound pattern as C4 — strengthens the paper's general warning rather than being a standalone result. If β>1 helps on RedCaps too, it's a genuine (if narrow) positive finding worth its own paragraph.
- **Cost:** 4 β values × 3 seeds = 12 runs, RedCaps-scale. `b_weight` is not an init-template key change requiring re-derivation logic beyond what's already implemented (config default confirmed at `configs/train/default.yaml:23`).

### Experiment 4 — CLIP fine-tuning baselines, run and reported

- **What:** Run `src/baseline/train_baseline.py` (modes `linear`, `last_blocks`, `lora`; `full` optional/lower priority given cost) on every dataset used in the paper, 3 seeds where feasible.
- **Why:** No baseline numbers exist anywhere in the repo (confirmed: no `res/baseline/` outputs, no report references `CLIPBaseline`/`train_baseline`). A main-venue reviewer's first question is "how does this compare to just fine-tuning CLIP" — currently unanswerable. This is infrastructure that was built (per `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-26-clip-baselines-design.md`) and never exercised for results.
- **Success criteria:** Not pass/fail — this is a required table for any venue, independent of what it shows. If CoSiR (any init) underperforms `lora`/`last_blocks` fine-tuning, that's a caveat to state plainly, not a blocker (the paper's contribution is about the initialization/graph-signal question, not "CoSiR beats fine-tuning").
- **Cost:** 3 modes × 3 datasets × (1–3 seeds) — the largest compute item in this plan if run at full seed count; acceptable to run `linear`/`last_blocks`/`lora` at 1 seed first and only replicate the numbers that matter for a close comparison.

### Experiment 5 — MS-COCO extension (stretch, contingent on 1–4)

- **What:** Repeat the signal-validation probes (lift/held-out-encoder check) and Experiment 1 (init-only comparison) on COCO. The pipeline is already dataset-agnostic (buddies init supports COCO per `2026-06-09_weekly_conditional_buddies.md` §3 "Impressions, MS-COCO, RedCaps").
- **Why:** COCO is the one dataset every reviewer already has priors about; a third, standard-benchmark data point substantially strengthens any generalization claim and is close to required for the stretch (main-track) tier.
- **Gate:** Only pursue if Experiments 1–4 land on schedule by the Week-3 checkpoint (§6) and (ideally) Exp. 1 is positive — otherwise the marginal value of a third data point on an already-established negative/nuanced result is lower than spending the time on writing.

### Experiment 6 — Approach C: does cross-VLM survival predict downstream usefulness? (stretch, optional)

- **What:** The 16-cell cross-VLM survival study (`2026-07-16_buddy_cross_vlm_survival.md`) explicitly deferred this: *"whether higher cross-VLM survival translates into better downstream condition-vector initialization or training... deliberately left as future work."* This would connect the paper's two halves (signal generalizes / does using it help) directly, e.g. by testing whether samples whose buddy edges are in the high-consensus core benefit more from buddy init than samples whose edges are low-consensus.
- **Gate:** Only if ahead of schedule after Experiments 1–4 (and 5 if pursued). This is a new sub-study, not a rerun of existing tooling, and carries the highest risk of scope creep in this plan.

### Experiment 7 — Causal tightening of the near-duplicate confound (cheap, high value — do if time allows after 1–4)

- **What:** Re-run Family #2's confirmed Impressions setting (`λ_con=1.0`) with same-source-photo edges explicitly excluded from the buddy graph, and check whether the win (+2.3 t2i / +3.2 i2t) shrinks toward the RedCaps number (+0.4 / −0.9).
- **Why:** The current confound evidence (§8d of the progress report) is correlational — 40.6% same-photo enrichment coincides with the win. Directly removing that structure and watching the effect shrink turns "we believe this explains it" into "we removed it and the effect disappeared," a substantially stronger claim for reviewers.
- **Cost:** Cheap — reuses the existing `identity_stats` probe (`src/test/20260622_buddy_analysis/buddy_analysis.py`) to build an edge mask, one training arm (λ_con=1.0, same-photo-excluded graph) × 3 seeds, compared against the already-measured λ_con=1.0 full-graph result. No new infrastructure.

### Experiment 8 — Buddy-init encoder-pair ablation (added 2026-08-24; stretch, gated on 1–4 landing on schedule)

- **What:** `compute_buddy_init(img_feats, txt_feats, ...)` (`src/conditional_buddy/compute_buddies.py`) is a pure function over raw feature arrays — it has no dependency on which model produced them. Every run to date feeds it CLIP img/txt features via `FeatureManager`. This experiment swaps in each of the 16 (vision × text) encoder pairs already cached for the cross-VLM survival study (`heldout_feats/redcaps/*.npy`, `src/test/20260716_buddy_cross_vlm/`) as the *init source*, holding the frozen CLIP backbone, gated combiner, and all training-time buddy terms (off) fixed — i.e., Exp. 1's `buddies` arm with encoder-pair id as the swept axis instead of `{imgtxt, buddies}`.
- **Why:** Tests whether the specific encoder pair used to build the buddy graph/init matters for downstream retrieval usefulness (as opposed to C2/C3, which only tested whether the graph *structure* survives encoder changes). Joining each pair's Exp. 8 retrieval Δ against its already-measured C3 cross-VLM survival rate also answers, for free, the question Experiment 6 explicitly deferred ("does higher cross-VLM survival predict downstream usefulness") — Experiment 6 can likely be dropped from scope once this runs.
- **Tooling:** New sweep script mirroring `scripts/run_buddy_seeds.sh`, with encoder-pair id as the swept axis instead of `initialization_strategy`; feeds pre-cached alternate features into `compute_buddy_init` in place of CLIP's `FeatureManager` output. New analysis mode (sibling to `analyze_buddy_families.py`) keyed on encoder pair, joined against the C3 survival-rate table for the secondary correlation check. Sweep/analysis outputs (per-pair buddy-init tensors, results tables) live in a new dated folder, `src/test/20260824_buddy_init_encoder_ablation/`, per the repo's existing debugging-folder convention — kept separate from the raw feature caches below.
- **Feature storage:** The 150k pass reuses the existing `heldout_feats/<dataset>/<model>.npy` cache as-is (`src/test/20260708_heldout_grid/extract_heldout.py`, single source of truth via its `cache_path()` function, already imported by `cross_vlm_buddy.py`) — untouched, since C3's report already depends on those exact paths. The 300k extension needs a **new re-extraction pass**: the 6 held-out encoders (dinov2, siglip_v, vit_sup, minilm, bge, e5) were only ever cached at 150k, so 300k features don't exist yet. These go in a new sibling directory, `heldout_feats_300k/<dataset>/<model>.npy`, with `cache_path()` extended to take an optional `scale` argument (default preserves current 150k behavior) rather than hand-duplicating paths. This keeps the cache easy to locate (two fixed directories), reusable (one function, no hardcoded paths), and safely deletable (`heldout_feats_300k/` is self-contained and nothing else depends on it, unlike the 150k dir — `rm -rf` it once Exp. 8's 300k report is written).
- **Scope:** RedCaps only (the clean, unconfounded C5/C6 cell), all 16 (vision × text) pairs from C3, 3 seeds per pair (48 runs). CLIP backbone, combiner, and all training-time buddy terms fixed throughout — only the init-source encoder pair varies. Build and validate the pipeline at 150k first (matches C5's scale, reuses existing feature cache, no extraction cost); extend the same sweep to 300k afterward, since that is the scale where a CLIP-baseline comparison is meaningful (C6) — this extension additionally requires re-running held-out feature extraction for all 6 encoders at 300k before the sweep itself can start.
- **Success criteria:** Informative either way, not pass/fail. If one or more non-CLIP pairs beat the CLIP-sourced init, that's a stronger initializer candidate to lead the paper with. If all 16 cluster near the current CLIP-sourced result, that is itself evidence that the *graph structure* — not the specific encoder — is what drives usefulness, strengthening C2/C3's "not an encoder artifact" claim from a new (downstream-use) angle rather than only the signal-validation angle those claims currently rest on.
- **Cost:** 48 runs at 150k (~100-epoch schedule) to build and validate the pipeline — no new feature extraction needed. A further 48 runs at 300k if the 150k pass is informative enough to justify the extension, plus a one-time 300k re-extraction pass (6 encoders × 1 dataset) as a prerequisite. Largest single compute item added to this plan — gated the same way as Experiments 5/7 (pursued only if 1–4 land on schedule).

### Experiment 9 — What predicts buddy-signal strength across RedCaps subreddits? (cheap, independent — run anytime)

- **What:** `subreddit_lift()` (`src/test/20260623_redcaps_buddy/redcaps_buddy.py`) already computes per-subreddit lift but only reports the top-15 most-enriched. Extend it to every subreddit with enough samples, then correlate per-subreddit lift against three subreddit-level properties: size (sample count), caption diversity (mean pairwise text-embedding distance, or vocabulary size, within the subreddit), and visual homogeneity (mean pairwise CLIP image-embedding distance within the subreddit).
- **Why:** C1's validation story currently rests on one aggregate ~20× lift number for RedCaps. A per-subreddit breakdown explained by measurable properties (e.g., "signal is strongest in visually homogeneous, caption-diverse subreddits") upgrades C1 from "the signal exists on average" to a mechanistic account of *when* it's strong — more citable, and more interesting to a TMLR reviewer, than a single global number. This deepens the signal-validation claim (C1); it is not a re-measurement of the downstream training-use question (C5/C6).
- **Tooling:** Reuses existing `subreddit_of`, `subreddit_lift`, and cached RedCaps CLIP features — no new graph-building infrastructure. New one-shot analysis script producing a per-subreddit table plus a correlation/regression against the three properties, and a scatter figure.
- **Success criteria:** Not pass/fail — a deliverable table/figure regardless of outcome. A clean correlation with one or more properties is a positive addition to C1's narrative; a null correlation is still useful (rules out these three properties as confounds/explanations of signal strength).
- **Cost:** Cheap — no training, no new feature extraction. Days, not compute; independent of every other experiment in this plan, so it can run in parallel with anything, including Experiment 0.

### Experiment 10 — Modality-provenance-aware distance mixing (typed edges) (added 2026-08-24; stretch, gated on a cheap diagnostic that already ran positive)

- **What:** `compute_buddy_init` builds the union buddy graph `E = A_img ∪ A_txt`, then computes both an image-cosine-distance and a text-cosine-distance for *every* edge of `E` and blends them with a fixed `alpha*D_img + (1-alpha)*D_txt`, regardless of which modality(ies) actually support that edge (`mix_distances`, `src/conditional_buddy/buddy_graph.py`). A no-training diagnostic (`src/test/20260824_buddy_graph_disagreement/diagnose_disagreement.py`, run on real RedCaps 150k and 300k data, both scales agreeing) found that **only ~1.6–1.9% of edges are cross-modally confirmed ("both")** — the other **~98%** are single-modality-only (57% img-only, 42% txt-only) — and for those edges, the fixed blend collapses a genuinely good supporting-modality rank (median 0.22–0.30, where 0 = best) down to **median 0.50–0.51 — statistically indistinguishable from a random pair** — while ~80% of nodes are "bridge" nodes connecting to different neighbors via different, non-overlapping modalities (the structural precondition for spectral false-transitivity). This experiment adds an opt-in `distance_mode: "typed"` (default stays `"blend"`, the current behavior, fully unaffected) that uses each edge's own supporting modality's rank alone for img-only/txt-only edges, keeping the blend only for `both` (no disagreement to correct) and `repair` edges (added by `ensure_min_degree`/`ensure_connected`, owned by neither modality) — then runs the same paired-ablation pattern as Experiments 1/8 to test whether this changes retrieval, and specifically whether it narrows C6's still-open gap to raw CLIP (`test_pre_diff`, already logged automatically by every eval call in `src/eval/pipeline.py` — no new eval code needed).
- **Why:** A literature review (multi-view/multiplex graph fusion: MVN2VEC, HAN, R-GCN, co-regularized/conflicting-views spectral clustering — MvKDR, CI-GMVC) found the general principle "preserve per-view provenance rather than blending disagreeing views into one weight" well-established elsewhere, but confirmed this project's exact compound failure (union of two per-modality mutual-kNN graphs + fixed global blend + resulting bridge-node risk, specifically for a one-shot spectral training initializer) is not directly addressed in prior work — see the diagnostic's own module docstring for the full citation trail. Given C6's central open finding — buddy-init narrows but does not close the retrieval gap to raw CLIP — a construction step that is empirically shown to collapse ~98% of the graph's edges to near-random before the spectral step ever runs is a concrete, previously-undiagnosed, and cheaply-fixable candidate explanation, not a speculative one.
- **Tooling:** New `distance_mode` parameter threaded through `compute_buddy_init`/`mix_distances` (or a sibling function), reusing the diagnostic's own edge-classification logic (`classify_edges`, already tested against synthetic cases including a hand-verified dilution direction). New `train.buddies.distance_mode` config key, added to the buddies template-compatibility `_extra` dict in `train_cosir.py` (same integration pattern as Experiment 8's `encoder_pair` and the existing `b_weight`) so a stale template from one mode is never silently reused under the other. New sweep script mirroring `scripts/run_init_ablation.sh`/`run_buddy_init_encoder_ablation.sh` (`distance_mode ∈ {blend, typed}` as the template-key axis, 3-seed Hydra multirun inner loop), and a new analysis script reading `test_oracle/*` (retrieval) and `test_raw`/`test_pre_diff` (already-instrumented CLIP-baseline comparison, per C6's precedent) from wandb.
- **Scope:** RedCaps-150k only (matches C5's scale, cheapest iteration), same fixed operating point as C5/C6 (`lr=1e-3, lr_label=1e-4, embedding_dim=16, alpha=0.5`), `initialization_strategy=buddies` fixed, all training-time buddy terms off (isolating the init-construction effect, same discipline as Experiment 1). 2 arms (`blend`, `typed`) × 3 seeds = 6 runs. Does not touch the frozen CLIP training backbone (init-construction-only change) — same non-violation of §9's backbone boundary as Experiment 8, and not a new training-time loss mechanism (§9's "no Family #4" boundary applies to loss terms during training, not to how the init graph's edge distances are computed).
- **Success criteria:** **Positive** (`typed` beats `blend` on `test_oracle` retrieval, seed-replicated, mean/SEM ≥ 2, AND narrows the `test_pre_diff` gap to CLIP) → a genuine, previously-undiagnosed methodological improvement, worth its own paragraph/ablation table in the paper and a candidate to become the new default. **Null** (no reliable retrieval difference) → the dilution is real (diagnostic-confirmed) but doesn't matter for the downstream spectral embedding's usefulness — still a legitimate, citable negative result (a graph-construction flaw that doesn't propagate to a measurable training effect). **Negative** (`typed` is worse) → investigate briefly (e.g., whether the diluted blend was accidentally acting as a regularizer), but not a blocking finding either way — report as found.
- **Cost:** 6 runs at 150k (~100-epoch schedule) — same shape and cost as the cheapest cell of Experiment 1. The diagnostic itself (already run, no additional cost) is what justifies spending this compute rather than treating the finding as diagnostic-only.

## 5. Statistical methodology (standardize across all new experiments)

These are not new conventions — they're what the project already does in `2026-06-24_buddy_progress_report.md` §8 and `2026-07-16_buddy_cross_vlm_survival.md`; this section exists so every new experiment (1–4, 7) is held to the same bar as the existing ones, since a reviewer will read them side by side.

1. **≥3 seeds for every comparison that feeds a paper claim.** The single-seed grid in the original Family #1/#3 ablation was demonstrably misleading (see §8a of the progress report) — this is not a hypothetical risk, it already happened once in this project.
2. **Paired-within-seed reporting**: mean Δ ± std across seeds, plus `mean/SEM` as the significance read, consistent with the existing convention (e.g. `mean/SEM = 7.0`, `z = +11`).
3. **A measured noise floor, not a zero baseline.** The progress report established the noise floor (~0.1–0.7 R1) from a duplicate-config run; any new experiment claiming a real effect should be checked against that floor, not against Δ=0.
4. **Analytic nulls over Monte Carlo where a closed form exists** — the project already made this switch once (`7226e5b perf: replace Monte-Carlo permutation null with closed-form analytic null`); no new experiment in this plan needs a new null construction, but if one comes up, prefer the closed form.
5. **No silent cherry-picking of operating points.** Where a sweep is involved (Exp. 2, 3), report the full curve, not just the best cell — the progress report's own peak-finding (λ_con dose-response, §8b) is the template: show the rollover, not just the peak.
