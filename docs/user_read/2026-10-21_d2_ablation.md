# The instructed graphs carry most of our model's gain; without the caption terms, style comes close to the instructed Qwen image

> 2026-10-21 · [Full report](../reports/auto/percept/2026-12-22_d2_ablation.md)

## Summary

- We changed our model's parts one at a time, on development paintings, with 3 seeds per variant.
- The aspect graphs carry most of the gain: without their pairs, their structure or their instruction, style AMI
  falls by 0.15 to 0.17.
- The pull toward the instructed Qwen image (the teacher) adds little once these graphs are in place.
- Without the caption terms, emotion rises a little and style clearly. One such variant reaches style 0.3945, close
  to the instructed Qwen image's 0.3952. The cause is not yet known.
- With no instruction anywhere in training, our model falls below PercepT core on all three aspects.
- An instruction moves a view's k-means AMI much more than its linear probe accuracy.

## How we got here

We want one model that finds emotion, style and genre clusters in paintings. Each of these is an aspect.
Our model reads a painting's Qwen image embedding, made without an instruction (the default image).
Qwen3-VL-Embedding is a vision-language embedding model; with an instruction that names an aspect, it gives an
instructed image instead.
Our model passed its emotion test against PercepT core, our version of the published PercepT topic method.
On style and genre it stays below the instructed Qwen image.
You asked for this step because "we did not really understand our current design".
It answers two questions: which parts carry the gain, and how the four Qwen image embeddings differ.

## Which parts carry the gain?

**Problem.** The recipe came from many small development choices, so we did not know what each part brings.

**Idea.** One shared layer feeds one head per aspect; a second network reads the painting's caption.
Three kinds of loss terms train it:

1. **Buddy terms:** two paintings that are neighbours in an aspect graph are pulled together.
   Each graph comes from an instructed Qwen view. For emotion and style, the content similarity is subtracted first
   (the content correction).
2. **Own-caption terms:** each image is pulled toward its own caption.
3. **The teacher:** the head output is pulled toward the instructed Qwen image of its aspect.

We trained 12 variants on 3 seeds each. Most change one part; a few remove a group of terms or combine two changes.
We scored them on 5,000 development paintings: paintings from the training side, used to shape the method.
Genre has labels on 4,053 of them. Held paintings, kept for tests, were not touched.

AMI (adjusted mutual information) measures how well k-means clusters agree with labels.
An effect is a variant's AMI minus our model's.
Its 95% interval comes from resampling the paintings and seeds (a bootstrap).
A first check reran our model and matched earlier runs bit for bit.

**Did it work?** Yes, on style clearly; on emotion and genre the effects are smaller.
The 5,000 paintings chose our model in an earlier step, so removals may look worse than they are.
All of this is development data, with one dataset and one backbone (the Qwen model).

**Key evidence.** Effect on AMI against our model (0.0962 emotion, 0.3697 style, 0.4540 genre).
Italic values have an interval that crosses zero ("not resolved").

| Change | Emotion | Style | Genre |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Random pairs, same number per painting | −0.0296 | −0.1702 | −0.0625 |
| One default-view graph for all heads | −0.0222 | −0.1681 | −0.0287 |
| No buddy terms | −0.0214 | −0.1473 | −0.0449 |
| Teacher only (no buddy or caption terms) | −0.0169 | −0.0995 | −0.0194 |
| No content correction | −0.0066 | −0.0929 | *−0.0049* |
| No teacher | *−0.0032* | −0.0106 | *+0.0063* |
| Default-view teacher | *−0.0041* | *−0.0048* | *+0.0077* |
| Separate heads, no shared layer | *−0.0006* | *−0.0008* | *+0.0049* |
| No own-caption terms | +0.0055 | +0.0180 | *+0.0063* |
| Image endpoints only | +0.0057 | +0.0248 | *−0.0086* |

How to read the sizes: across its three seeds, our model's AMI varies by 0.0046 (emotion), 0.0082 (style) and
0.0128 (genre), from the highest seed to the lowest.

- The top three rows have a clear cost on all three aspects: the graph pairs, their structure and their instruction.
- The shared layer and the teacher change little; dropping the teacher costs style a small amount.
- On genre, the two teacher rows kept models from their first epoch, so their genre effects say little.
- The last two rows are the surprise: without the caption terms, emotion rises a little and style clearly.

The caption result has two caveats.
First, our model was chosen on these same paintings, so a variant that beats it here is a lead to check elsewhere.
Second, without the caption terms the teacher's weight grows from a fifth to a third of the total loss.
We cannot yet tell which change raises style.
"Image endpoints only" keeps just the image-to-image buddy term and the teacher; its caption network gets no loss.

## Does our model need Qwen's instruction, and is its input the limit?

**Problem.** The instruction enters training in two places: the graphs and the teacher.
A model with no instruction at all would work with any backbone, which would make a simpler story.

**Idea.** Four variants answer this:

1. The teacher uses the default image, so only the graphs carry the instruction.
2. One graph from the default view serves all heads, so only the teacher carries the instruction.
3. No instruction anywhere: the default-view graph and no teacher.
4. Instructed inputs: the model reads the instructed images, to see if the default input limits it.

**Did it work?** In these data the instruction acts mainly through the graphs; the teacher matters little.
With no instruction anywhere, our model falls below PercepT core on all three aspects; we ran no test of this.
Early stopping kept that variant's first-epoch model, which limits what it shows.
The default-view graph variant, whose best epochs were 2 to 4, is also below PercepT core on all three.
Instructed inputs raise style a little and leave genre at our model's level, below their own input.
So the default input is not the main limit, at least for genre.

**Key evidence.** Absolute AMI on the same development paintings; the Qwen and PercepT rows have no training.

| Model | Emotion | Style | Genre |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Our model | 0.0962 | 0.3697 | 0.4540 |
| Default-view teacher | 0.0921 | 0.3648 | 0.4617 |
| One default-view graph | 0.0739 | 0.2015 | 0.4254 |
| No instruction anywhere | 0.0733 | 0.1848 | 0.4241 |
| Instructed inputs | 0.0993 | 0.3857 | 0.4545 |
| PercepT core | 0.0772 | 0.2286 | 0.4359 |
| Default Qwen image | 0.0696 | 0.2217 | 0.4486 |
| Instructed Qwen image | 0.0929 | 0.3952 | 0.4956 |

On genre, our model sits close to the default Qwen image and well below the instructed one.

## How do the four Qwen views differ?

**Problem.** The instruction is where the style and genre gain comes from, but we did not know what it changes.

**Idea.** We compared the four image embeddings of the same 5,000 paintings: the default one and the emotion, style
and genre ones.
For each one and each label, we measured k-means AMI and a linear probe.
A linear probe is a logistic regression trained on the embedding to predict the label; we report balanced accuracy.
We also compared the views with each other by CKA, a similarity of two embedding spaces (1 for the same space).
Last, we looked at the offset: a painting's instructed embedding minus its default one.

**Did it work?** Yes, as a description; no intervals or tests.

- Each instruction raises its own aspect's AMI much more than its probe accuracy.
- The emotion and style instructions lower the other aspects' AMI; the genre instruction also raises style.
- On the image side, the four views stay similar (CKA 0.55 to 0.80).
- The three offsets share many directions, style and genre most.
- With the emotion or style instruction, image and caption agree less (by CKA) than with none; with genre, a bit more.

**Key evidence.** Image side, 5,000 development paintings.

| View | Emotion AMI | Emotion probe | Style AMI | Style probe | Genre AMI | Genre probe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Default | 0.0696 | 0.227 | 0.2217 | 0.530 | 0.4486 | 0.778 |
| Emotion instruction | 0.0929 | 0.240 | 0.1678 | 0.463 | 0.4096 | 0.751 |
| Style instruction | 0.0630 | 0.228 | 0.3952 | 0.560 | 0.3490 | 0.778 |
| Genre instruction | 0.0701 | 0.224 | 0.2644 | 0.532 | 0.4956 | 0.789 |

Chance for the probe is 0.111 (emotion), 0.037 (style) and 0.100 (genre).

## Advice

<details>
<summary>Our advice (open after giving your reading)</summary>

Our reading:

- The instructed graphs are the core of the method; beside them, the teacher is close to redundant.
- Without the caption terms, emotion and style rise, but we do not know why yet.
  Fewer caption terms and a larger teacher share are both possible causes.
- "Any backbone" needs Qwen's instructed graphs at training; we claim nothing for other backbones yet.
- Genre is the open problem, and its input is not the limit.
- An instruction changes which paintings sit close together more than what a linear probe can read.
- How sure: fairly sure of the graph and teacher readings; the caption reading is a lead.

Our recommendation:

- First, check the two caption-free variants against our model on development paintings that did not choose it.
- Add our model with a larger teacher share. This helps separate the two causes.
  For image endpoints only, a second change stays mixed in: its image-to-image term also weighs more.
- It costs under an hour if a second development sample can be drawn (not checked).
- If retrieval needs captions, prefer "no own-caption terms". It keeps the caption network, and its caption AMI
  rises on emotion and style (no interval).
- Then genre: first a probe of how much instructed structure the default image holds, then heads that follow the
  teacher more closely. Then the other backbones and ArtELingo-28.

## Glossary

- **Our model:** D2 (row R0); a shared layer, one head per aspect, buddy and caption terms, and the teacher.
- **Variant:** one ablation row (R1 to R12).
- **No own-caption terms, image endpoints only:** R6 and R7, the two caption-free variants.
- **Default and instructed Qwen image:** the Qwen embedding of a painting, made without or with an aspect instruction.
- **Aspect graph:** pairs of paintings that are close in an instructed Qwen view; Gres, Fres05 and R0o in the full report.
- **Buddy terms:** loss terms that pull the two paintings of a graph pair together.
- **Teacher:** the pull toward the instructed Qwen image (direct distillation).
- **Teacher share:** the teacher's weight as a part of the total loss.
- **Content correction:** content similarity subtracted before the graph is built.
- **AMI:** adjusted mutual information, the agreement between clusters and labels.
- **Not resolved:** the effect's 95% interval crosses zero, or the effect is within the seed range.
- **CKA:** linear centred kernel alignment, a similarity of two embedding spaces.
- **Backbone:** the frozen embedding model our model reads; here Qwen3-VL-Embedding.
- **Retrieval:** finding paintings from a text query; the 10 November gate needs it by aspect name.
- **Development and held paintings:** development paintings shape the method; held paintings stay unseen until a test.
