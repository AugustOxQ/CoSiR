## What exists

### NNCLR (nearest-neighbor positives in SSL)

Dwibedi et al., *"With a Little Help from My Friends: Nearest-Neighbor Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations"* (ICCV 2021, arXiv:2104.14548). NNCLR samples the nearest neighbor of an anchor's own embedding from a support set — a FIFO queue of recent embeddings, not a graph — and treats that neighbor as an additional positive in a single-modality (image-only) contrastive loss, replacing/augmenting the usual same-image-different-augmentation positive pair. It reports a real accuracy win (71.7% → 75.6% ImageNet linear eval) directly attributable to the neighbor-as-positive substitution. It is a training-time contrastive-loss mechanism, not an initialization scheme, and operates entirely within one modality.

### Mean-shift / prototype-based SSL (MSF, SwAV)

MSF (Koohpayegani et al., ICCV 2021, arXiv:2105.07269) shifts an anchor's embedding toward the mean of its k=5 nearest neighbors (drawn the same way as NNCLR, from a memory queue, not a graph) using a BYOL-style online/target encoder pair; with k=1 it collapses to BYOL exactly. SwAV (Caron et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.09882) does not use neighbors or a graph at all — it maintains a small bank of trainable prototype vectors and enforces cross-view consistency of soft cluster assignments to those prototypes ("swapped prediction"). Neither method builds an explicit graph structure (both use a queue/prototype bank instead), and neither is cross-modal — both are single-modality (image) SSL pretraining methods, and both use the neighbor/prototype signal as an ongoing training-time loss, not as a one-time initialization.

### Graph-Laplacian / spectral embedding init precedent (node2vec, recsys)

node2vec (Grover & Leskovec, KDD 2016) is a graph representation-learning method in its own right — biased random walks feed a skip-gram objective to produce node embeddings; it is the whole method, not an initializer for something else. Closer precedent is LEPORID (*"Initialization Matters: Regularizing Manifold-informed Initialization for Neural Recommendation Systems,"* arXiv:2106.04993) — Laplacian eigenmaps computed on a user-item interaction graph are used to endow trainable user/item embeddings with multi-scale neighborhood structure before neural training begins, with adaptive regularization for long-tail nodes; downstream neural recommenders initialized this way outperform both KNN and their randomly-initialized counterparts. This is the one clear precedent for "graph structure → initialization of a trainable per-node embedding, then trained normally" rather than the graph being the whole method. It is single-domain (a bipartite user-item graph), not cross-modal, and the graph is standard k-NN/interaction-based, not a mutual-kNN intersection built independently in two separate embedding spaces.

### CMFG — cross-modal mutual-kNN graph construction (closest match found; 2026-08-11 addition)

*"Mutual contextual relation-guided dynamic graph networks for cross-modal image-text retrieval"* (Scientific Reports, 2025; PMC12484662 — journal-hosted full text confirmed by direct fetch; no arXiv ID located, not verified against arXiv). CMFG builds k-NN graphs **independently within the image embedding space and independently within the text embedding space**, then draws a cross-modal edge between an image node and a text node only when they hold a **mutual (bidirectional) nearest-neighbor relationship**, evaluated within their own modality. This is, structurally, the same graph-construction primitive as conditional-buddies: cross-modal, per-modality-independent construction, mutuality required. It diverges entirely on *use*: the graph is not consumed once at init — it is rebuilt/consumed on every forward pass through GCN message-passing with attention-weighted edges, over trainable (not frozen) ViT/BERT encoders. It is a continuous architectural component baked into the retrieval model, closer in spirit to Family #2's training-time contrastive-supervision use of the buddy graph (the mechanism C4 found confound-driven) than to conditional-buddies' validated init-only use.

### Cross-lingual mutual-NN self-learning — Artetxe 2018 / MUSE (second-closest construction match; 2026-08-15 addition)

Artetxe, Labaka, Agirre, *"A Robust Self-Learning Method for Fully Unsupervised Cross-Lingual Mappings of Word Embeddings"* (ACL 2018, aclanthology.org/P18-1073 — verified) initializes an iterative self-learning procedure by finding word pairs that are **mutual nearest neighbors between two independently-trained monolingual embedding spaces** (source language, target language) — the same independent-per-space, mutual-NN construction primitive as conditional-buddies, located independently of the CMFG lineage and in a field with no other connection to vision-language retrieval (bilingual lexicon induction). Conneau et al., *"Word Translation Without Parallel Data"* (MUSE, ICLR 2018, arXiv:1710.04087 — verified) uses the identical mutual-NN dictionary-building step inside its own Procrustes self-learning refinement loop (distinct from CSLS, which is an asymmetric hubness-correction similarity metric, not a mutual-NN criterion — the two are easy to conflate in this paper but only the dictionary-building step is structurally relevant here). Both diverge from conditional-buddies on the load-bearing axis: the mutual-NN correspondence step is **re-invoked every refinement iteration**, alternating with a closed-form Procrustes/SVD mapping update, so the dictionary is never frozen after a single use the way conditional-buddies' buddy graph is. Also single-modality (two languages, not image/text), aligns a shared linear map rather than initializing a per-sample trainable vector, and has no held-out-encoder robustness analogue.

### Checked and ruled out — CV retrieval, clustering, hashing, zero-shot lineage (2026-08-15 addition)

A further adversarial pass checked four more lineages built on mutual/reciprocal-NN or graph-to-embedding patterns, all low-overlap. **k-reciprocal re-ranking** (Zhong, Zheng, Cao, Li, CVPR 2017, arXiv:1701.08398 — verified) builds reciprocal-NN sets in a single gallery embedding space and re-ranks retrieval results at inference time, recomputed per query — no cross-space construction, no init-time use. **Consensus-Driven Propagation** (CDP; Zhan, Liu, Yan, Lin, Loy, ECCV 2018, arXiv:1809.01407 — verified) builds kNN graphs from a committee of different face-recognition models on single-modality data and reconciles them into training-time pseudo-labels — the closest surface analogue located anywhere in this search to conditional-buddies' held-out-encoder robustness check (multiple independently-built graphs compared for agreement), but used for continuous pseudo-label generation rather than a one-shot init check discarded after validation; worth flagging as the closest *validation-methodology* analogue to date, distinct from the closest *construction* analogue (CMFG) or *init-use* analogue (Artetxe/MUSE, LEPORID). **Graph-regularized/GCN cross-modal hashing** (e.g. GCH, Xu et al., IJCAI 2019, and related variants) builds a single modality-unified affinity graph — not independent per-modality graphs intersected on mutuality — consumed continuously via GCN regularization; functionally covered by the existing CMFG entry, not a new pattern. **Graph-based zero-shot class-embedding init** (Wang, Ye, Gupta, CVPR 2018, arXiv:1803.08035 — verified; confirmed as the dominant ZSL-GCN paradigm by survey arXiv:2112.10006) runs a GCN over a single taxonomy graph to directly output classifier weights in one pass — the graph computation *is* the whole mechanism, not a graph used to seed a separately-trained frozen-backbone parameter. None of these four branches changes the yellow verdict.

### Supporting / adjacent context (2026-08-11 addition; extended 2026-08-15)

A few further items don't change the verdict but sharpen the framing: Balestriero & LeCun (*"Contrastive and Non-Contrastive Self-Supervised Learning Recover Global and Local Spectral Embedding Methods,"* NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2205.11508 — verified) show contrastive/non-contrastive SSL objectives are implicitly kernel spectral-embedding methods (ISOMAP/Laplacian eigenmaps in disguise); this is supporting theory for *why* a spectral/graph-derived initialization is a principled starting point, not a competing method. DINOv2 (arXiv:2304.07193) runs explicit copy-detection dedup as a data-curation step, confirming near-duplicate contamination of SSL pipelines is a known general concern in the field — which means C4's contribution is best framed as *quantifying* that known concern for a specific neighbor-as-positive mechanism and specific datasets, not as discovering that duplicates matter from scratch. CODER (arXiv:2404.17753) and DN²A (CVPR 2023, cross-*domain* not cross-*modal* neighbors) were also checked and are low-overlap (no graph, or no init, or wrong axis). CoOp/CoCoOp-family prompt-learning methods were checked for graph-based initialization of their learned prompt vectors and use word-embedding-derived init in every located variant — no overlap on the init mechanism. A 2026-08-15 pass additionally checked Similarity Network Fusion (Wang et al., Nature Methods 2014 — partially verified only, full text paywalled and corroborated via secondary sources): fuses independently-built per-view kNN graphs via iterative cross-diffusion over ~20 rounds, but with no mutual-NN construction criterion and no init-time use (the fused graph is the method's output, not a seed for downstream training) — low overlap, cite only if reviewer-defensiveness is desired. Co-training (Blum & Mitchell) and CCA-family multi-view methods were also checked and ruled out entirely — no located variant intersects two independently-built per-view kNN graphs via a mutual criterion; these combine views through joint objectives or projections instead.

## Differentiation

**Corrected 2026-08-11:** the original claim here — that no located prior work builds a mutual-kNN graph independently across two modality-specific embedding spaces — is false as stated; CMFG (above) does exactly that. The defensible novelty claim narrows accordingly, from "nobody builds this graph" to a conjunction that is still not matched by anything located: conditional-buddies (1) uses this cross-modal mutual-kNN construction (not unique — CMFG shares it, and per-modality-independent-then-intersected construction is now a known primitive that must be cited); (2) used **only once**, to set the initial value of a per-sample trainable condition vector, with the backbone **frozen** and **no ongoing graph-based loss** in the validated part of the project (C1–C3) — CMFG, NNCLR, and MSF all keep the graph/neighbor signal alive throughout training over trainable encoders, and LEPORID's graph-to-init precedent is single-domain, not cross-modal; (3) validated for robustness by **rebuilding the graph with encoders the model never sees** (6 held-out encoders, 16 cross-VLM pairs) — no located work in this space runs an equivalent cross-encoder reconstruction check. Property (2)+(3) together, not property (1) alone, is the load-bearing novelty claim: a cross-modal mutual-kNN graph, consumed exactly once as an initializer for a frozen-backbone model, with the graph's stability independently verified across encoder families. NNCLR and MSF remain the closest *training-time* analogues; CMFG is now the closest *construction-mechanism* analogue and must be cited explicitly wherever the graph-construction step is described, not just wherever the C4/training-time discussion is; LEPORID remains the closest *init-time* analogue but stays single-domain.

**2026-08-15 second pass (cross-lingual/multi-view and CV retrieval/clustering/hashing/zero-shot lineages):** found a second independent instance of the same shape — Artetxe 2018 / MUSE's mutual-NN dictionary-building step between two independently-trained embedding spaces, in bilingual lexicon induction, a field with no other connection to vision-language retrieval — and it diverges from conditional-buddies on the identical axis CMFG does: the mutual-NN mechanism is re-invoked every refinement iteration, never frozen after one use. Counting this pass, four prior-art items now share the construction primitive or a close cousin of it (CMFG, NNCLR/MSF's training-time neighbor use, LEPORID's single-domain-only init, Artetxe/MUSE's iterative re-invocation), and every one of them fails to match on property (2)+(3) — the graph is never both frozen-after-one-use and held-out-encoder-validated. That the divergence axis keeps holding across two independent adversarial passes, in fields as different as recsys, cross-modal retrieval, and bilingual lexicon induction, is itself evidence the narrowed novelty claim is the right one to stake the paper on, rather than an artifact of an incomplete first search. Separately, Consensus-Driven Propagation (CDP, face clustering) is the closest located analogue to the *validation methodology* itself (multiple independently-built graphs from different models compared for agreement), even though its downstream use — continuous pseudo-label generation — has nothing to do with initialization; citing it preempts a reviewer asking "why not compare to multi-view consensus clustering" when justifying the held-out-encoder robustness check.

## Connection to the C4 finding

Yes — this is a legitimate, citable counterpoint, and CMFG sharpens rather than weakens it. C4 found that using the buddy graph as ongoing contrastive supervision (the NNCLR/CMFG-shaped use of this signal: keeping the graph alive as a training-time mechanism) produces an apparent win on Impressions that is substantially explained by a near-duplicate confound (40.6% same-source-photo edges, 279× enriched) and does not replicate on RedCaps. Neither NNCLR-class papers nor CMFG report auditing whether "nearest neighbor in embedding space" edges are secretly near-duplicate frames or crops of the same source image — a check this project made only because the two datasets available (Impressions' repeated-photo structure vs. RedCaps' near-absence of duplicates) made the confound visible by comparison. As DINOv2's dedup step shows, the field already knows near-duplicates *can* contaminate SSL pipelines in general; C4 is the specific, measured instance of that generic concern for the neighbor-as-positive/neighbor-as-continuous-graph-signal family specifically — a citable data point, not a reason to soften NNCLR's or CMFG's own reported results (different data, different task, no evidence of an equivalent confound there). The project has the instrumentation (identity_stats, the same-photo-exclusion probe planned as Experiment 7) to make the point rigorously rather than as speculation.

## Verdict

**Overlap risk: yellow** (unchanged tier — now stress-tested by two independent adversarial search passes, 2026-08-11 and 2026-08-15)

Related but distinguishable, on a narrower basis than originally stated: CMFG shares conditional-buddies' cross-modal mutual-kNN graph-*construction* primitive and **must be cited and explicitly differentiated** in the paper wherever that construction is described — presenting it as unprecedented would be a citable gap a cross-modal-retrieval-literate reviewer catches immediately. Artetxe 2018 / MUSE independently share the same *mutual-NN-across-two-independently-built-spaces* primitive from an unrelated field (bilingual lexicon induction), reinforcing that the construction step itself is not the novel part. The core claim survives because none of these — CMFG, NNCLR/MSF, or Artetxe/MUSE — ever freeze the graph/neighbor signal after a single use over a frozen backbone: CMFG and NNCLR/MSF keep it alive as a continuous training-time mechanism, and Artetxe/MUSE re-invoke it every refinement iteration. Conditional-buddies uses it exactly once, to initialize a frozen-backbone per-sample vector, with that choice independently stress-tested by rebuilding the graph from held-out encoders — a combination (init-only + frozen-backbone + cross-encoder robustness check) nothing located across either search pass matches. LEPORID remains the closest single-shot init-time precedent (single-domain, not cross-modal). A broader sweep of CV retrieval/clustering/hashing/zero-shot-init lineages (k-reciprocal re-ranking, consensus-driven face clustering, graph-regularized cross-modal hashing, zero-shot GCN class-embedding init) turned up nothing closer than CMFG on construction or Artetxe/MUSE on init-use; the one item worth a defensive citation from that sweep is CDP (Zhan et al. 2018), whose committee-of-models graph-agreement check is the closest located analogue to the held-out-encoder validation methodology, independent of its unrelated downstream use. The paper's related-work section must now cite and differentiate against five works (CMFG, LEPORID, NNCLR, MSF/SwAV, Artetxe 2018 + MUSE/Conneau 2018 as a paired citation), plus CDP as a defensive citation for the validation methodology specifically. Leaning on init-only/frozen-backbone/cross-encoder-robustness as the actual novelty axis rather than the graph-construction mechanism itself remains the right call — that axis has now survived two independent adversarial search passes spanning contrastive SSL, graph-Laplacian recsys init, cross-modal dynamic graphs, cross-lingual alignment, and CV retrieval/clustering/hashing/zero-shot lineages, with no located counterexample.
