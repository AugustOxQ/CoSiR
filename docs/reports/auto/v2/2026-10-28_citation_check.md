# Citation check of the CVPR plan and its two literature reports

**Report date:** 2026-10-28 (sequence date in this folder; the check was run on 2026-10-02).
**Mode:** ARS v3.22.2, `academic-paper` citation-check mode (`citation_compliance_agent`), run read-only. We did not edit the checked documents, so the agent's auto-correction step became a list of proposed corrections.
**Checked documents:**
- the plan, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-cosir-v2-cvpr-publication-plan-design.md`;
- the [CVPR literature review](../../literature/2026-10-21_cvpr_literature_review.md) (the "review");
- the [aspect task novelty check](../../literature/2026-10-24_aspect_task_novelty_check.md) (the "novelty report").

## 1. Verdict

Every work the plan cites exists under the title, authors and venue given. Every number the plan attributes to a paper matches that paper's own table. One factual error reaches a statement in the plan, and three citations need a precise source before they go into the paper.

**Error that affects a claim in the plan.**
- **E1, plan §5.3 (ArtGAN genre names).** The plan says the WikiArt genre class-name file "is gone upstream" and that id 5 (`nude_painting`) "is inferred by elimination and alphabetical order". In fact the file is still upstream as `WikiArt Dataset/Genre/genre_class`, without a `.txt` extension. Only the `genre_class.txt` path returns 404. The file lists all 10 genres. It matches the 9 ids recovered in `src/test/20261026_genre_coverage/genre_coverage.json` and gives id 5 as `nude_painting`.
  - **Correction:** "The id-to-name mapping is ArtGAN's `WikiArt Dataset/Genre/genre_class` file; it agrees with the mapping recovered from ArtELingo-28 on all 9 shared ids." Drop "inferred by elimination". The genre decision itself does not change.

**Citations in the plan that are correct but need a precise source.**
- **C1, plan §5.4 and §8 (SEARLE 14.4).** The plan calls SEARLE 14.4 a "published frozen ViT-B/32 result". The SEARLE paper (ICCV 2023) reports no GeneCIS result: it evaluates FashionIQ, CIRR and CIRCO. The 14.4 is CIReVL's re-run of SEARLE at ViT-B/32 (CIReVL Table 3, per-task 18.9 / 13.0 / 12.2 / 13.6). CIReVL's ViT-L/14 SEARLE row also averages 14.4 (17.1 / 16.3 / 12.0 / 12.0), so the paper must name the backbone.
  - **Correction:** "SEARLE 14.4 (ViT-B/32, as re-run by CIReVL, Table 3)".
- **C2, plan §5.4 (OSrCIR reproduction 14.0).** The 14.0 is correct, but it exists only in Paracosm v1 (Wang, Zhao, Kong, arXiv 2602.00813v1, Table 3, OSrCIR with GPT-4o, reproduced). The current version (v6, 1 July 2026, accepted to ECCV 2026) has no GeneCIS result at all; its reproduction of OSrCIR there covers CIRCO and FashionIQ. The review's reference entry links the unversioned page, which now opens v6. The same applies to the review's Paracosm 16.1 and to its trivial B/32 baselines (11.5 / 8.4 / 12.7).
  - **Correction:** cite "arXiv 2602.00813v1, Table 3", or drop the reproduction from the paper.
- **C3, plan §8 (SpLiCE as a basis for both modalities).** SpLiCE's method (§4) is defined for CLIP image embeddings. Its Appendix B.3 applies SpLiCE representations to both retrieval directions on MSCOCO, so using it on captions has a precedent in the paper itself.
  - **Correction:** cite App. B.3 when the plan applies SpLiCE to captions, and state the centering used for each modality.

**Errors in the reports that do not reach the plan.**
- **E2, novelty report §3.1 and Table 1 (Contextual Visual Similarity).** The report says the method weights "fixed AlexNet fc7 features" with "a non-negative weight per feature dimension". The paper uses "L2 normalized activations from the pool5 or fc7 layer of VGGNet". Its weights are W = diag(w), learned per query by gradient descent and initialised to an all-one vector, with no non-negativity constraint (the loss uses ‖Wx‖², so only w² matters). The plan's own sentence ("per-query weights from 1 to 5 examples") is correct: k positive and k negative images, k ∈ {1, 3, 5}.

**Counts.** We checked 35 citations: the 23 the plan's claims depend on, plus 12 other works the plan names.
- 32 verified, 10 of them with a caveat recorded in their row;
- 2 errors (E1, E2);
- 1 unverified: Rocchio 1971, whose book chapter we could not open.

§3.3 lists seven open items: the Rocchio chapter, five sub-claims inside verified rows, and the ArtGAN TIP paper behind row 30. The roughly 140 other entries in the two reports' reference lists were not re-checked in this pass.

## 2. Method

**Tools.** WebSearch and WebFetch both worked; no hook blocked egress. We opened:
- arXiv abstract pages, arXiv HTML and ar5iv full texts;
- CVF open-access records;
- the JMLR and NeurIPS proceedings PDFs and the KISSME PDF from TU Graz;
- Hugging Face model cards and GitHub repositories.

We extracted text from five PDFs locally with pypdf (RCA, KISSME, Xing et al., SpLiCE and Contextual Visual Similarity) and read the relevant passages directly.

**Raw-text rule.** WebFetch answers through a small summarising model, and it was wrong three times in this run:
- its first read of CIReVL Table 3 mixed the ViT-L/14 SEARLE row into the ViT-B/32 row;
- it gave the Xing et al. authors in the order of the NeurIPS web page, while the printed header reads Xing, Ng, Jordan, Russell;
- it reported that SpLiCE never decomposes text, which Appendix B.3 contradicts.

We therefore reported no error until we had confirmed it in raw text: a full verbatim table copy, a raw file, or locally extracted PDF text. Two candidate errors, the SEARLE per-task numbers and the SpLiCE modality claim, did not survive that check.

**Format checks.** The three documents are planning documents. They cite by author, venue and arXiv id, not in a citation style, so the agent's APA format checks (et al. rules, DOI form, ordering) do not apply. We used the agent's cross-check differently: we listed works the plan relies on that have no reference entry in either report (§3.4), because they will become missing references in the paper.

## 4. What this check does not cover

We checked the 23 priority citations and 12 further works the plan names. The remaining roughly 140 entries in the two reports' reference lists are out of scope. Most are named only in the reports' surveys and are not used by any claim in the plan, so they were not re-checked here. Their status is whatever the reports themselves recorded, including the reports' own UNVERIFIED list (review §6, last paragraph). We also did not test the reports' novelty conclusions ("we found no paper that ..."), because a citation check can confirm what a paper says but not that no other paper exists.
