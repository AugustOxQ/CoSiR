# Buddy Stage 1 silhouette gap: ranked next directions

**Research memo, 26 September 2026.** No new experiment was run. Rankings estimate expected *decision value* per implementation and verification cost, rather than the chance of producing a large silhouette in isolation. The five distinct directions below cover all five requested formulations: feeding frozen buddy embeddings to PercepT and training sequentially are the **same experiment** when the buddy encoder stays frozen (candidate 2).

## What the existing results actually establish

Attention-h1's four-seed held-out means are emotion AMI **0.1241**, genre AMI **0.2406**, and silhouette **0.0397**; 3/4 seeds pass the predeclared AMI Pareto bar. Cosine scheduling without embedding noise raises mean silhouette to **0.0466**, with 3/4 seeds still passing. The schedule-plus-Leiden-pseudo-contrastive variant reaches **0.0739** silhouette and **0.3011** genre AMI, but emotion AMI falls to **0.1160** and 0/4 seeds pass. Thus even the buddy variant with visibly better separation has already demonstrated a downstream-relevant trade-off, not a free geometric gain. [Consolidated report, §§2–3](2026-09-26_artelingo_buddy_vs_percept_stage1.md); [baseline stress](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_baseline_seed_stress_pilot_report.md); [pseudo-contrastive stress](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_noise_schedule_pseudo_contrastive_stress_pilot_report.md).

The faithful PercepT replication reports **0.5120** held-out silhouette in its **128-D DEC latent** at seed 42, but also labels that run **collapsed**: 50/67 surviving held-out centers receive fewer than 1% of points, and the emotion AMI **0.1092** misses the Pareto bar despite genre AMI **0.3288**. Its reconstruction loss is present, but at the final checkpoint it is about **0.000080** versus KL **0.391414**. Those numbers do not prove reconstruction gradients are strong enough to prevent cluster occupancy collapse. The later balance-loss pilot retains reconstruction yet worsens the collapse and AMIs. The report's “missing reconstruction anchor” is a **plausible hypothesis about the buddy hybrid**, not a demonstrated sufficient remedy or proof that PercepT's high silhouette means useful, well-populated topics. [Faithful recipe report, Phase 1 and decision](pilots/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/percept_stage1_faithful_recipe_pilot_report.md); [balance report](pilots/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/percept_stage1_faithful_recipe_balance_pilot_report.md); [consolidated report, §4](2026-09-26_artelingo_buddy_vs_percept_stage1.md).

The displayed silhouette gap is also **not a controlled like-for-like metric**. Buddy scores are sampled on a 32-D unit sphere using Leiden labels (the baseline re-clusters held-out data independently); PercepT's score uses its 128-D unconstrained latent and surviving train-fitted DEC-center labels. Buddy's score uses a seeded at-most-6,000 draw followed by a 4,000-point silhouette sample; PercepT's evaluator scores the full held-out latent. The validated Stage 2-compatible buddy labels instead come from k=20 transfer onto the **19 train communities**, improving held-out AMIs to **0.1364/0.2530** at seed 42. Silhouette must be recomputed with these transferred labels before using it as a Stage 2 argument. [Baseline stress method](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_baseline_seed_stress_pilot_report.md); [held-out label transfer](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/heldout_label_transfer_pilot_report.md); [PercepT evaluator](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage1_pilot.py).

## Ranked candidates

### 1. Decide on the Stage 2 objective with a matched audit and a small image-only mapper comparison

**Mechanism.** First recompute both systems' held-out silhouette with the same sampled paintings, distance definition, sampling seed, and each system's *deployable* frozen-train labels: k=20 transferred Leiden labels for buddy, surviving fitted centers for PercepT. Report cluster occupancy, emotion/genre AMI, and silhouette together. Then fit a small matched image-only mapper using the cached patch features and frozen Stage 1 targets. Compare the two target systems on a **common external outcome**, such as a fixed-capacity probe from predicted topic scores to held-out human emotion labels (and genre with its much smaller sample), alongside a patch-feature-only control. Per-system topic AUC can diagnose predictability, but it cannot be the deciding cross-system score: predicting 19 mostly hard communities and 40 multi-hot DEC targets are different tasks. This directly asks whether the putative silhouette deficit damages the use Stage 2 actually has for topics.

**Why plausible.** Buddy's competitive AMI and 3/4-seed Pareto clearance, the pseudo-contrastive silhouette/emotion trade-off, and the k=20 shared-vocabulary success all weaken the case for optimizing silhouette on its own. Conversely, AMI is only a proxy: the current Stage 2 pilot uses a 40-wide BCE classifier with argmax-plus-`q > 2/40` multi-hot DEC targets, whereas buddy currently supplies 19 hard communities. Nothing tonight established that the latter train an equally good image-only mapper. PercepT's high silhouette could reflect its DEC assignments and center collapse rather than Stage 2 utility. [Consolidated report, recommended solution and §6](2026-09-26_artelingo_buddy_vs_percept_stage1.md); [Stage 2 target construction](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage2_pilot.py); [label-transfer report](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/heldout_label_transfer_pilot_report.md).

**Smallest pilot.** The matched silhouette/occupancy audit on saved embeddings is the cheap first gate. If it leaves a consequential question, use one frozen seed-42 target set per method, the existing patch cache, identical mapper capacity and training budget, and a prespecified common held-out human-label probe; only then repeat promising results across the buddy baseline's four seeds. Keep train/held-out identities and probe-fitting splits separate. **Cost/risk:** audit low; mapper comparison medium, chiefly target construction and fair evaluation. **Negative result means:** if buddy maps poorly on a common outcome, competitive Stage 1 AMI is insufficient and topic geometry or target quality deserves work. If it maps as well despite low matched silhouette, silhouette parity has little demonstrated value for this use; it would still be inaccurate to claim buddy “beats PercepT” on geometric separation.

### 2. Feed frozen Attention-h1 vectors into PercepT's own autoencoder + reconstruction + DEC (requested candidates 1 and 4)

**Mechanism.** Fit Attention-h1 normally on train paintings, freeze it, compute aligned train and held-out **32-D L2-normalized** vectors, then use those vectors as PercepT's input `h`. Fit a fresh PercepT autoencoder by reconstruction pretraining, initialize 100 K-means centers on its clean latent, run the faithful joint KL + MSE phase, retain 67 centers, and apply the frozen encoder and centers to held-out vectors. This is exactly the two-stage/sequential proposal; “feed buddy embedding as PercepT input” and “freeze buddy, then fit PercepT” are **not independent candidates** unless one instead fine-tunes the buddy encoder jointly, which is a different, higher-risk ablation. Freezing makes the result interpretable and avoids the InfoNCE/DEC gradient conflict. [Faithful recipe implementation](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage1_faithful_recipe_pilot.py); [Attention-h1 implementation](src/test/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/run_learned_student_arch_sweep_pilot.py).

**What “unmodified” really permits.** PercepT's current input is **2,816-D**, not a buddy embedding: two separately L2-normalized CLIP ViT-B/32 image/text vectors (512+512), concatenated as 1,024-D content and L2-normalized; that content is included **twice** beside a separately L2-normalized **768-D** masked-token-mean, then caption-mean, GoEmotions-RoBERTa affect embedding; the full `(content, content, affect)` concat is L2-normalized and cast to float32. The duplicate content gives a 2:1 content:affect norm budget. Attention-h1 instead consumes **train-fitted 50-D PCA content** and **28 GoEmotions probabilities**, using linear projections, one-head attention, LayerNorm and L2 normalization. The two inputs therefore differ in both information and geometry. [Base PercepT input and autoencoder](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage1_pilot.py); [content construction](src/test/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/run_cca_audit_pilot.py); [Attention-h1 architecture](src/test/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/run_learned_student_arch_sweep_pilot.py).

PercepT's `build_autoencoder(input_dim)` already parameterizes only its first encoder and last decoder widths: **input→500→500→2000→128** and **128→2000→500→500→input**. Thus replacing `h` by 32-D arrays requires **no internal layer redesign**, but the boundary feature-loading/reporting path must change and the network is now **overcomplete (128 latent > 32 input)**. It is not a scientifically trivial swap. A unit-vector 32-D target has much larger mean per-coordinate energy than a unit-vector 2,816-D target, so the raw MSE and effective strength of `lambda_R=1` change; the 0.1 latent-noise scale and 100/67 center recipe also need to be audited, not silently called equivalent. Do **not** rescale 32-D vectors merely to make a raw MSE number look like the old one; record per-feature MSE, baseline reconstruction, KL, gradient sizes, and center occupancy.

**Why plausible.** The detached cluster-head retry gave only **0.0018** mean silhouette in its own isolated latent, while PercepT supplies the missing reconstruction pathway and pretraining. The existing PercepT code is the quickest way to test the *paired objective* without reinventing it or damaging buddy's learned representation. It also tests whether buddy's compressed signal is still sufficient for DEC. [Detached-head report, summary](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_decoupled_cluster_head_detached_pilot_report.md); [consolidated report, §4](2026-09-26_artelingo_buddy_vs_percept_stage1.md).

**Smallest pilot.** One frozen seed-42 Attention-h1 snapshot, one PercepT faithful Variant A run, and matched evaluations of (a) original buddy Leiden labels on the original 32-D vectors, (b) new DEC labels in the new 128-D latent, and (c) those same DEC labels back in the original 32-D vectors. Include no-DEC autoencoder pretrain and the original 2,816-D PercepT run as controls; screen seed 42, then use multiple seeds only for a noncollapsed AMI-and-silhouette improvement. **Cost/risk:** medium implementation and verification; the main risks are an overcomplete AE, changed loss/noise scale, label-count mismatch, and adding PercepT's full complexity to a proposed simpler replacement. **Negative result means:** if reconstruction is good but DEC labels collapse, reconstruction alone is insufficient under this 32-D input and 100/67 recipe; it does **not** refute reconstruction on a better target or a different cluster count. If 128-D silhouette rises but 32-D silhouette and AMIs do not, the gain is a private DEC geometry, not evidence that buddy's original representation improved.

### 3. Widen `D_SHARED` without changing the objective

**Mechanism.** Run the same one-head, two-teacher Attention-h1 with `D_SHARED` at 64 and 128, keeping the 50-D PCA content, 28-D affect probabilities, losses, temperature, graph construction, Leiden recipe, and training budget fixed. This probes representation capacity independently of DEC. The reference comparison is PercepT's **128-D unconstrained latent** versus buddy's **32-D unit sphere**, but width alone changes neither buddy's objective nor its spherical geometry. [Architecture source](src/test/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/run_learned_student_arch_sweep_pilot.py); [PercepT autoencoder source](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage1_pilot.py).

**Why plausible, and why uncertain.** Attention-h1's logged effective rank at 95% **grew from 12 at initialization to 20 of 32** in the architecture sweep, while the top covariance eigenvalue fraction fell substantially; the schedule trajectories likewise reach roughly 17–19 rather than collapsing. This is evidence *against* a hard 32-D bottleneck, though the remaining dimensions may still carry fine separation. Four attention heads underperformed one head, and widening the per-view MLP underperformed its smaller predecessor; neither is a controlled test of wider **shared** representation. [Architecture sweep report](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/learned_student_arch_sweep_pilot_report.md); [schedule trajectories](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_noise_schedule_pilot_report.md).

**Smallest pilot.** Paired seed-42 32/64/128 runs, logging effective rank, top eigen fraction, teacher-graph recall, both held-out AMIs, cluster occupancy, and matched-protocol silhouette; stress an apparent winner at seeds 7, 123 and 2024. **Cost/risk:** low-to-medium; straightforward dimensions but extra training and graph evaluation. **Negative result means:** width alone is unlikely to explain the gap under this two-InfoNCE objective. It says nothing decisive about a reconstruction-anchored latent.

### 4. Add a genuinely reconstruction-anchored clustering head to Attention-h1 (the report's fifth attempt)

**Mechanism.** Replace the KL-only 32→32 unconstrained `ClusterHead` with a proper encoder/decoder around its cluster latent; pretrain it to reconstruct a **clean, frozen 32-D fused buddy vector**, initialize K-means only afterward, then jointly optimize reconstruction and DEC KL while keeping the buddy trunk frozen for the first diagnostic. That is the smallest controlled repair of the exact detached-head failure. Reconstructing the *same cluster latent* would be an identity target and no useful anchor. Reconstructing the **fused input vector** anchors the head, though it preserves only information that survived Attention-h1. A later, more expensive joint variant could reconstruct the actual student inputs through separate heads—**50-D train-PCA content and 28-D affect probabilities**, with each target standardized or loss-weighted separately—while allowing gradients into the buddy trunk. That supplies an information-preservation pressure upstream. Reconstructing PercepT's 2,816-D raw fused input would additionally require its different 768-D affect encoder and asks a 32-D buddy student trained on 28 probabilities to recover information it never saw; it is a poor first test. [Detached head implementation](src/test/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/run_attention_h1_decoupled_cluster_head_detached_pilot.py); [PercepT input construction](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage1_pilot.py).

**Why plausible, with a necessary qualification.** The un-detached and detached decoupled heads both collapsed their post-prune labels in 4/4 seeds and had near-zero own-latent mean silhouette (**−0.0062** and **+0.0018**); detaching changed fused-space silhouette only **+0.0039**, falsifying the original gradient-leak diagnosis. An anchor is the clearest untested structural difference. Yet the literal Euclidean buddy DEC's KL was only roughly **0.003–0.012** against two InfoNCE terms of roughly **5 each**; even the vMF retry, which corrected silhouette's sign, missed both AMI bars in 4/4 seeds. Loss magnitude and center count are confounders. `lambda_R=1` in PercepT means *one coefficient within* `KL + MSE` on a 2,816-D unit vector; it cannot simply be copied into `InfoNCE_content + InfoNCE_affect + lambda_DEC·KL + lambda_R·MSE` on 32-D or 78-D targets. With a frozen trunk, compare KL and reconstruction **gradients on the head's shared encoder**, their training trajectories, and a no-reconstruction ablation; with an unfrozen trunk, also compare each against both InfoNCE gradients. Pick a small prespecified coefficient range that makes reconstruction consequential without swamping the two teacher losses. [Euclidean DEC report](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_dec_hybrid_pilot_report.md); [vMF DEC report](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_vmf_dec_hybrid_pilot_report.md); [decoupled reports](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/attention_h1_decoupled_cluster_head_pilot_report.md).

**Smallest pilot.** Use the same frozen seed-42 buddy snapshot and 100/67 center convention as the detached head. Compare its KL-only baseline to reconstruction-pretrained `KL + weighted MSE` using the **32-D fused input as target**, measuring reconstruction error, gradient ratio, 128- or 32-D cluster-latent silhouette, 32-D fused-space silhouette under the same DEC labels, occupancy, and both held-out AMIs; stress only if all material criteria improve. This partly overlaps candidate 2, but a small head isolates the proposed anchor while PercepT's full AE tests the established recipe. **Cost/risk:** medium-to-high; new decoder, pretraining phase, scale calibration, and additional failure modes. **Negative result means:** the simple fused-vector reconstruction anchor does not rescue KL-only collapse at this cluster count. It does not rule out reconstructing upstream content/affect or a different clustering objective, but four prior failures make further unrestricted DEC tuning low value.

### 5. Preserve buddy's train-vocabulary topic uncertainty for Stage 2, if hard labels prove limiting

**Mechanism.** Convert the k=20 neighbor vote over frozen train Leiden communities into a calibrated `[N, K_train]` vote-frequency or distance-weighted distribution, instead of sending only an argmax hard label to the mapper. This targets the actual mismatch with PercepT Stage 2's multi-hot targets without requiring DEC or silhouette parity. Train topics remain fixed; held-out labels use only the frozen train vocabulary. A decision threshold for multi-hot targets can be chosen on train data, with argmax always included, matching the intent of PercepT's target construction while making the changed semantics explicit. [Held-out transfer report](pilots/20260923_artelingo_buddy_analysis/heldout_label_transfer_pilot_report.md); [PercepT Stage 2 target function](src/test/20260922_percept_topic_pipeline/run_percept_stage2_pilot.py).

**Why plausible.** k=20 transfer improves both held-out AMIs and covers all 19 train communities, but its validated output is hard labels only; the current Stage 2 pilot constructs multi-hot soft-assignment targets and a 40-wide head. The nearest-neighbor vote already contains some ambiguity information that argmax discards. **Smallest pilot:** at seed 42, compare one-hot labels, vote-frequency targets, and a sparse multi-hot threshold on the *same frozen buddy embeddings and patch mapper*, recording label cardinality, class occupancy, topic predictability, and the common external outcome from candidate 1; repeat only if the difference is material. **Cost/risk:** low-to-medium, with calibration and label semantics the principal risks. **Negative result means:** softening buddy's targets does not solve any Stage 2 gap; it does not imply that higher latent silhouette is required.

## Recommendation and stopping rule

**Start with candidate 1.** Its inexpensive matched audit prevents optimizing an apples-to-oranges silhouette, and its small Stage 2 comparison tests the project's actual use for the labels. Treat **noncollapsed occupancy plus held-out AMI plus a common downstream outcome** as a joint gate; a high silhouette alone is insufficient, as PercepT's faithful run and the failed balance pilot show. If that gate shows buddy needs more separable topics, candidate 2 is the cleanest geometry experiment: it uses the existing paired reconstruction+DEC implementation and freezes the buddy encoder, so a result can be attributed to the second stage. Candidate 3 is a cheap independent capacity check. Candidate 4 is reserved for a specific failure of candidate 2 or a desire to keep clustering inside a buddy-derived architecture. Candidate 5 is relevant only if hard-vocabulary supervision is the bottleneck. The five ranks are therefore about expected **information and usable improvement per cost**, not a prediction that any DEC variant will reach 0.51.
