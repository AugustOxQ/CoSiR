## Section I — Where we left off (~2026-07-16), 2-3 sentences

CoSiR extends a **frozen** pretrained CLIP model (the image/text encoders are
never fine-tuned) with a small trainable **condition vector** per training
sample. That condition vector is fed into a small trainable **combiner**
module that nudges one side's CLIP embedding (image or text) before the
usual contrastive retrieval loss is computed — the goal being retrieval that's
better than raw CLIP while staying close to CLIP's original embedding space.
Each sample's condition vector starts from an initialization derived from the
**buddy graph**: a graph connecting samples that are mutual nearest-neighbors
in CLIP's image space, text space, or both — the intuition being that samples
which CLIP already considers close to each other should start with similar
condition vectors, rather than random ones. This buddy-graph idea and its
validation (does it actually work, is it an artifact of CLIP specifically,
etc.) is what the returning member already knows; do not re-explain the buddy
graph's own internal mechanics (spectral embedding math etc.) beyond this.

## Section II — Two months of hardening the foundation (mid-July → early Sept), condensed

Keep this section SHORT — a recap paragraph plus a compact table, not an
experiment-by-experiment retelling. Source facts (all verified, use exactly
these):

- The buddy graph was confirmed **encoder-agnostic**: rebuilding it from 16
  different vision/text encoder pairs still recovers a coherent, largely
  overlapping structure — it's a general property of CLIP-like embedding
  spaces, not a fluke of one specific CLIP checkpoint.
- **Buddy-graph initialization was confirmed to beat a generic initialization**
  on RedCaps (a real, positive retrieval effect, larger at 300k samples than
  at 150k) and became the project's standing default going forward
  (`initialization_strategy=buddies`). Neither initialization beats plain,
  unconditioned CLIP retrieval outright at this scale — buddy-init is "the
  better of two initializations," not "beats CLIP."
- A long series of robustness/diagnostic checks (which specific encoder pair
  builds the graph, how graph edges are weighted, whether the graph confuses
  indirectly-related samples for directly-related ones, per-subreddit signal
  strength) all came back either null or confirming the existing design was
  sound — **none of them changed the architecture**, so they're skipped here
  in detail; the graph construction most of Experiment 18 relies on is the
  same one validated across all of this.
- **`K` (the neighborhood size used to build the buddy graph) was found to
  need scaling with dataset size, not staying fixed.** The project-wide
  default (`K=30`) turned out to be measurably suboptimal at 500,000 samples:
  a predicted, larger value (`K≈39`) produced a real retrieval win
  (image-to-text Recall@1 +0.97) over the old default. This is a genuine
  open finding — the default in the config files had not yet been updated
  as of this work — mention it as "still open," not resolved.
  (Do NOT invent a resolution — just state it's flagged, unresolved as of
  this report.)
- **The fusion module (the "combiner" that mixes a condition vector into a
  CLIP embedding) was overhauled.** The original combiner was two MLP towers
  feeding a learned gate — heavier than it needed to be for mixing a
  16-dimensional condition signal into a 512-dimensional embedding. A
  literature-grounded redesign proposed several lighter alternatives; the
  strongest, a **rank-16 "low-rank residual adapter"** (the condition vector
  produces a small additive correction to the embedding, starting at exactly
  zero correction before any training), was ablated and won cleanly on the
  main retrieval metric — a result that **held up, and by an even larger
  margin, when re-validated at full training scale** (500k samples, 100
  epochs) rather than just a small smoke test. This low-rank adapter is the
  combiner Experiment 18 is built on. (It is explicitly selected for
  Experiment 18's runs, not yet the global default in every config file —
  say this plainly, don't imply it's fully rolled out everywhere.)
- **Conditioning is asymmetric by design**: the condition vector is only
  fused into one side's embedding (the image side, by the project's standing
  default, `combine_side="img"`); the other side (text) passes through a
  separate, small linear layer (`other_proj`) that starts as an exact
  identity function (changes nothing) and is free to adjust slightly during
  training. This asymmetry is a deliberate, load-bearing design choice, not
  an oversight — mention in one sentence that a parallel, not-yet-merged line
  of work found evidence that this one-sidedness may be responsible for a
  retrieval asymmetry seen between the two directions (image→text vs.
  text→image retrieval), as a forward-pointer, not a resolved fact.

## Section III — Architecture snapshot immediately before Experiment 18 (Diagram 1 goes here)

Describe in plain prose what "the previous model" is, matching Diagram 1
exactly (see diagram spec below): frozen CLIP encodes image and text
separately; a **large lookup table holds one trainable condition vector per
training sample**, initialized from the buddy graph and then trained directly
with its own (much larger) learning rate; the image-side embedding is
combined with that sample's condition vector inside the low-rank residual
combiner; the text-side embedding passes through the identity-initialized
`other_proj` layer; both final embeddings are trained with a contrastive
retrieval loss.

State the motivating problem that leads into Experiment 18, **in the words of
this framing** (this is the actual reasoning that motivated Experiment 18,
keep it faithful): a per-sample lookup table has no shared structure across
samples — every row is independent, so there's no natural way to ask "what
does this condition mean" in general, or to explain *why* two samples got
different treatment beyond pointing at the combiner/adapter math itself. It
also can't produce a condition vector for a brand-new, never-seen sample
without a separately-trained side network (`condition_predictor`) whose job
is to approximate the table. This motivates trying to replace the table with
something structured and inherently generalizable.

## Section IV — Experiment 18: prototype-pooled conditioning (idea → implementation → key results)

**Idea.** Replace the giant, independent per-sample lookup table with a small,
**shared bank of 16 learnable "prototype" vectors** — think of them as a small
set of learned archetypes. Every sample's condition vector becomes a
soft, weighted blend of these 16 shared prototypes, computed fresh from that
sample's own CLIP feature — not looked up from a table. The hope (the actual
design goal): (a) since there are only 16 shared archetypes instead of one
row per sample, the resulting condition space should naturally organize into
interpretable clusters, and (b) because it's a function of the sample's own
features rather than a per-sample table entry, it automatically works for new,
unseen samples with no separate predictor network needed.

**Implementation** (Diagram 2 goes here — same downstream combiner/other_proj/
loss as Diagram 1, only the condition-vector source changes): each of the 16
prototypes has a learned "key" and a learned "value" vector. For a given
sample, its own CLIP feature is projected into a **query**; that query is
compared against all 16 keys to produce 16 similarity scores; a
**temperature-controlled softmax** turns those scores into attention weights
that sum to 1; the sample's condition vector is the attention-weighted sum of
the 16 prototypes' **value** vectors. Everything downstream — the low-rank
combiner, `other_proj`, the retrieval loss — is completely unchanged from
Section III; only the condition vector's source changed.

**Key results — first pass, default settings, 3 random seeds, RedCaps-150k.**
State plainly and precisely:
- Retrieval: roughly on par with the previous (lookup-table) design — no
  clear win or loss on either retrieval direction, both designs sitting
  slightly below plain unconditioned CLIP retrieval at this data scale
  (consistent with an already-known pattern at this scale, not a new
  surprise).
- Interpretability — the actual point of this redesign — **failed outright**:
  in all 3 seeds, the learned condition space collapsed to **essentially 1
  effective dimension out of 16** (see "Effective dimensions" in the glossary
  below) — practically every sample ends up with nearly the same condition
  vector. Cluster-quality (silhouette score) was weak and inconsistent across
  seeds (one seed even scored *worse than random grouping*). A simple test of
  whether two chosen proxy properties of a photo (see "warmth"/"register"
  below) could be recovered from the condition space came back null or
  unstable — clearly worse than the old per-sample table, which reliably
  encoded both.
- A genuinely interesting side-finding: the one built-in health-check metric
  this design shipped with (**usage entropy** — see glossary) looked
  reassuring (fairly spread-out attention) even while the collapse above was
  happening. The reason: attention was spread-out-but-tilted — on 88–99.5%
  of all 150,000 samples, the single *strongest* prototype match was the same
  one or two prototypes, and because the softmax stayed close to uniform
  everywhere, the final blended output ended up nearly identical for nearly
  every sample regardless. Worth stating cleanly as a lesson: a "spread-out
  attention" metric and "diverse final output" are not the same thing, and
  this project's monitoring didn't originally check the second one directly.

**Root-cause fix.** The prototype bank's own parameters were quietly being
trained **1,000× slower** than the old per-sample table's learning rate — an
optimizer-configuration gap, not a fundamental flaw in the idea. Once the
prototype bank was given its own, correctly-scaled learning rate (and a
tunable initial "sharpness," i.e. starting temperature, for its attention),
a quick single-seed sweep showed a clear, monotonic trend: as the prototype
bank's learning rate increased, the collapse broke up — entropy sharpened,
attention concentration spread across more prototypes, and cluster quality
(silhouette) roughly quadrupled.

**Key results — best setting found, 3-seed confirmation, RedCaps-150k.**
State plainly and precisely, this is the most important result in the report,
get the numbers exactly right:
- Real, seed-consistent interpretability gains: silhouette score went from
  inconsistent/near-zero-or-negative (0.12, 0.31, −0.13 across the 3 original
  seeds) to consistently positive and much higher (0.55, 0.56, 0.70 across
  the 3 confirmation seeds) — the condition space broke out of its 1-D
  collapse; one of the two proxy properties ("register") became recoverable
  from the condition space in 2 of 3 seeds, for the first time in this whole
  investigation.
- A serious, seed-replicated cost: **image-to-text retrieval got clearly
  worse** — Recall@1 dropped to a mean of about 10.4, versus about 16.8 for
  the original (uncollapsed-fix) prototype design and about **17.8 for doing
  no conditioning at all** (plain CLIP). In other words, at this operating
  point, conditioning is actively worse than not conditioning at all, on this
  one retrieval direction. The other retrieval direction (text-to-image) was
  unaffected either way. This gap is described as tight and consistent across
  all 3 seeds (not seed noise).
- **Bottom line, state exactly this framing**: this is a genuine, real,
  seed-replicated *trade-off*, not a finished fix. It proves the underlying
  diagnosis (the learning-rate gap) was real and the interpretability
  problem is fixable in principle, but this specific operating point is not
  yet usable as a replacement for the old design. The next step (a smaller
  learning-rate value, or a different training mechanism entirely) is an open
  decision, not yet made.

## Section V — Glossary (careful, precise, hedge where the source material hedges)

Write short (2-4 sentence) plain-language entries for each term below. Do not
invent or embellish beyond what's stated — several of these terms
(`warmth`/`register` especially) have an important, deliberate hedge in the
source material; keep it.

- **Condition vector / conditioning** — (briefly, the returning member likely
  half-remembers this) the small trainable vector attached to each sample
  that steers its embedding.
- **Combiner (low-rank residual adapter)** — the small module that turns a
  condition vector into a small additive correction applied to a CLIP
  embedding, starting at exactly zero correction before training.
- **`other_proj`** — the small linear layer applied to the side of the pair
  that the condition vector does *not* touch; starts as an exact identity
  function.
- **Prototype bank / attention pooling** — the Experiment 18 mechanism: a
  small set of shared, learned vectors (prototypes), combined per-sample via
  softmax attention instead of a per-sample lookup table.
- **Oracle retrieval (Recall@1/5/10)** — retrieval accuracy measured by
  giving the model the best-available condition vector for each query — the
  standard way this project measures "how good could retrieval be with
  conditioning." Always compare against "raw" retrieval — plain, unconditioned
  CLIP — as the reference floor; report whether each design beats that floor,
  not only whether it beats the other design.
- **`warmth` / `register`** — two proxy groupings of RedCaps subreddits used
  to test whether the model's condition space separates meaningfully
  different kinds of photos. `warmth` contrasts companion-animal/cute-pet
  subreddits (cats, puppies, guinea pigs, ...) against curiosity-framed
  subreddits (mildlyinteresting, interestingasfuck, ...); `register`
  contrasts polished/professional photography subreddits (earthporn,
  foodporn, carporn) against casual-snapshot subreddits (mildlyinteresting,
  itookapicture). **Important, deliberate hedge — keep this**: a dedicated
  audit found `warmth` is mostly explained by plain photo content
  (animal-vs-not), not confirmed emotional tone, and `register` carries very
  little signal beyond content either. Treat both as "does the condition
  space separate these two subreddit groups" probes, not as validated
  measurements of emotion or writing formality.
- **Probe selectivity** — train a simple classifier to guess a sample's
  `warmth`/`register` group from nothing but its condition vector; selectivity
  is that classifier's real accuracy minus its accuracy on randomly shuffled
  labels (a control). Meaningfully positive = the condition space actually
  encodes that distinction; near-zero = it doesn't.
- **Effective dimensions (95% variance)** — how many independent directions
  are needed to explain 95% of the spread across a set of condition vectors.
  Close to the full width (16 here) means the space is using its full
  capacity in varied ways; collapsing to 1 means nearly every vector differs
  from every other along only a single shared direction.
- **Silhouette score** — a standard clustering-quality number: positive and
  high means clean, well-separated groups; near zero or negative means no
  real cluster structure (the latter can be worse than a random grouping).
- **Usage entropy vs. concentration** — usage entropy measures how spread out
  each sample's soft attention is over the 16 prototypes (this design's
  original, built-in health metric). It is not the same thing as whether the
  *final output vector* actually varies across samples — see the Section IV
  side-finding; this gap was the key thing this investigation's diagnostics
  had to add on top of the original metric.

## Section VI — Where it stands / what's next (short, 3-5 sentences)

State plainly: this is an honest, real, open trade-off, not a finished
result. The interpretability goal the redesign was built for shows genuine,
seed-replicated progress at the best setting found so far, but that same
setting currently makes one retrieval direction meaningfully worse than doing
no conditioning at all. Three directions are on the table for whoever picks
this up next: try a smaller value for the prototype bank's learning rate
(weaker interpretability gains were seen there, but the retrieval cost was
never checked); build a different training signal for the prototype bank
directly (e.g. a loss that explicitly pushes different conditions to produce
different outputs, or a loss that discourages the bank from collapsing onto
one or two prototypes); or treat the current finding as the recorded result
and move on. No decision has been made yet — this is presented to the team
for input.
